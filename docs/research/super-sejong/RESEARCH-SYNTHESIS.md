# Super Sejong Engine: Korean-Specific, Meaning-Preserving Revision

Research synthesis · 3 October 2026 (corrected 4 October; the review, measurements and engine revision are in [EVALUATION-2026-10-04.md](EVALUATION-2026-10-04.md)) · status: literature review, constructed probes, and a bounded service prototype. This is a research program with executable artifacts, **not** a claim of doctoral validation, a general Korean grammar checker, or measured improvement in readers' judgments.

## Abstract

The service rewrites Korean prose while trying to retain the writer's assertions, register, layout, and protected facts. Its central problem is an asymmetric one: a pleasing local phrasing change is low value if it changes who acted, whether an action occurred, whether it was required or merely possible, or the amount and unit of a claim. The proposed Super Sejong Engine therefore separates (1) source-anchored diagnostics, (2) candidate generation, (3) hard preservation checks, (4) uncertain-case abstention, and (5) genre- and audience-sensitive preference evaluation. Korean morphology and discourse structure motivate the design, but no individual surface cue proves semantic equivalence. The current implementation contributes deterministic guardrails and bounded style hypotheses; the larger morphology, discourse, corpus, and human evaluation components remain research work.

## Research questions and evidence boundary

**RQ1.** Which Korean-specific structures cause meaning drift in otherwise fluent revisions? Negation scope, modality, information structure, particles, ellipsis, honorification, numeric counters, and document-level reference are the primary candidates. **RQ2.** Which observed patterns are actual errors versus acceptable genre choices? Orthographic rules can identify some invalid forms, whereas translationese, nominalization, sentence length, and connective frequency usually require context. **RQ3.** Can source-aligned, selective revision improve naturalness without increasing factual or pragmatic errors? This requires blinded Korean-reader evaluation and a licensed, representative corpus; neither has yet been completed.

## Linguistic model and design hypothesis

The unit of revision is a **source span with a discourse neighborhood**, not an isolated suspicious word. Korean particles can signal arguments and information structure simultaneously. 은/는 is an auxiliary (topic or contrast) particle, not a case marker: it can sit on a subject, an object or an adverbial, so it does not fix a grammatical role by itself. Swapping 은/는 and 이/가 shifts topic, focus or contrast and is not automatically safe; replacing 은/는 with 을/를 is a role change only when the 은/는 phrase was the subject. (Corrected 4 October 2026; see [EVALUATION-2026-10-04.md](EVALUATION-2026-10-04.md), section 6.) 도 and 만 add meanings that a grammaticality checker may miss. A zero subject may be recoverable from preceding discourse, and inserting one can change focus. Sentence-final endings encode register and interpersonal force; subject, object, and addressee honorification are separate. Consonant/vowel and syllable principles explain written forms, but a pronunciation-based substitution is not an editorial rule. These distinctions motivate a feature ledger with explicit **unknown** values instead of forced parses.

For each aligned source/candidate span, the research schema is:

```text
SpanRecord = {
  source_offsets, genre, audience, speaker_or_quote,
  entities, quantities(value, unit), temporal_anchors,
  clauses: [predicate, participants(role, particle, confidence),
            polarity(cue, target, scope), modality, obligation,
            tense_aspect, conditions, attribution],
  discourse: [topic, referents, connective_relation, register],
  analysis_uncertainty
}
```

The record is a *proposed representation*, not a claim that the current regex implementation fills every field. Offsets must always refer to original text; morphology-normalized forms must never be concatenated to reconstruct it. Alignment should preserve sentence identity where possible and keep a non-alignment outcome when clauses split, merge, or cross references. A candidate is eligible only if protected anchors match, hard high-confidence semantic signatures match, document structure remains permitted, and uncertainty is low enough for that edit class. Formally, delivery is conditional on `anchors_ok ∧ structure_ok ∧ ¬known_drift ∧ uncertainty_below_policy`; an ambiguous candidate is preserved or surfaced for review. A good style score cannot override a known semantic failure.

## Super Sejong pipeline

1. **Prepare and classify.** Preserve original bytes, Unicode form, source offsets, Markdown/Office structure, code, citations, quoted speech, and document boundaries. Request or infer genre conservatively; “unknown genre” uses a limited editorial policy. A future optional morphology backend may provide POS and sentence spans, but the service must keep a dependency-free fallback and compare both on Korean names and unusual spacing.
2. **Diagnose locally.** Emit bounded, source-anchored candidates rather than global labels. Current TypeScript code marks two possible public-prose constructions. It excludes fences, list/headline/quote lines, inline code, and quoted literals; it does not auto-replace them. Repeated endings, collocations, and clause density are future diagnostics that require genre calibration. In particular, `자료를 통해 사실을 확인했다` is not intrinsically an error.
3. **Generate minimally.** Ask the model for exact before/after anchors or a controlled whole-text response, with the original facts and register fixed. Apply edits only to the declared source span. A no-edit answer is valid. Model warnings are bounded public notices rather than untrusted model text.
4. **Verify with independent invariants.** The current Python component checks signed quantities and recognized units, sentence count/order in Office, overt negation and some target movement, obligation/modality distribution, high-confidence participant role swaps, and two high-risk antonym pairs. Existing guards also protect quotes, names, Markdown structure, direct-text factual sentences, and Office package parts. Failed candidates retry conservatively or roll back to the source. These are *clear-drift detectors*, not a theorem of semantic identity.
5. **Resolve uncertainty and rank.** A future candidate ranker should operate **after** hard checks, compare no-edit and edited alternatives within the declared genre, and expose abstention. Bidirectional Korean NLI can be an additional signal: source⇒candidate tests for candidate-added claims; candidate⇒source tests for omitted source claims. Contradiction, neutral, and low-confidence outcomes need separate handling. Similarity and entailment models trained on translated or narrow tasks must be validated on our edit distribution.
6. **Check document continuity.** Re-evaluate quantities, referents, modality, naming, and register across adjacent spans and the full document before export. Office binary/package verification and text semantics are different guarantees. A passing file-format check does not establish faithful prose.

The division between hard gates and suggestions matters. `안 먹고 갔다` versus `먹고 안 갔다`, `세 달` versus `세 주`, `민수는 지수를 도왔다` versus a role-swapped clause, and `가능하다` moved between projects are high-risk contrasts that tests now exercise. `민수는` versus `민수가`, `한 시간` versus `1시간`, and a legitimate patient-focused passive illustrate why a universal string-preservation rule is also wrong. Other semantic flips remain possible: the finite high-risk antonym list cannot cover Korean vocabulary, implicit causation, or pragmatics.

## Evidence from reproducible local probes

The four scripts under [scripts](scripts/) are executable and disclose their constructed inputs. [C](scripts/C_morphology_probe.py) compares two locally installable analyzers on 14 designed strings; timing and sentence-boundary observations are **host- and sample-specific**. [I](scripts/I_corpus_probe.py) counts transparent features in 16 assistant-written texts, deliberately labeled *concrete* or *formulaic*, never *human* or *AI*. [K](scripts/K_polarity_probe.py) compares a cue-count baseline with an experimental clause/predicate signature on 36 hand-labeled, in-sample contrasts; its metrics can change when the product baseline changes. [S](scripts/S_readability_probe.py) computes descriptors for eight constructed passages and assigns no grade. These probes test feasibility and expose counterexamples. They have no random population sample, independent rater labels, or calibrated false-accept rate. They must not be used to advertise an accuracy or naturalness gain.

An isolated live Codex functional sample did exercise the new style hint: `자료를 검토하는 과정에서 오류를 발견했다.` became `자료를 검토하다가 오류를 발견했다.`, while the following sentence stayed intact; the two gate results were `GATE_OK` and its temporary workspace was empty afterward. This is one observed transformation, not a controlled demonstration that the hint caused the change or that Korean readers prefer it.

A later [paired service-path benchmark](../../reports/2026-10-03-super-sejong-blog-benchmark.md) processed 20 public posts sampled reproducibly from 28 prologue-visible entries on one Korean blog. Each current-service arm used the same source, model, effort, and one-chunk route; the ablation removed only Super Sejong style findings and four semantic checks. Both arms changed 17 final outputs. Super Sejong required 33 model calls and 13 retries, versus 29 and nine for the ablation; the median per-job times were 76.7 and 69.4 seconds respectively, although the within-post time difference varied in both directions. The full gate flagged four ablation outputs on replay and no Super Sejong outputs; these flags are not adjudicated meaning errors, and some triggering spans are plausible contrastive paraphrases. Only one input elicited a style finding, so this study provides little evidence about naturalness. A constructed, one-mutation-per-post negation probe found an actual coverage gap in the contracted negative copula `아닙니다`: the original guard caught 14/20 deliberate flips and the corrected guard caught 20/20. A separate live observation exposed a false polarity alarm for the spacing correction `안됩니다 → 안 됩니다`; the guard now counts both as negative surfaces. These are targeted corrections, not population-level sensitivity or specificity estimates.

## Falsifiable evaluation program

The primary estimands must be reported together: the intention-to-edit difference in blind native-reader naturalness across **all attempted passages**, the rate of semantic or pragmatic harm, and the abstention/source-preservation rate in each genre and audience. A conditional naturalness score among only meaning-preserved outputs can be useful diagnostically, but selecting those outputs after seeing the result would exaggerate the apparent benefit. Additional safety outcomes include missed changes in negation/participants/units/modality, over-rejection of valid edits, and latency. Report all by genre and document length. Detector AUC is not an outcome for this service.

Build a licensed evaluation set with provenance, permission scope, version and hashes. Proposed strata include public notices, workplace reports, essays, customer messages, and dialogue/literary excerpts; each needs factual, register, and no-edit controls. Hold out writers, prompts, topics, source collections, and model families. Build adversarial minimal pairs for short/long negation, prohibition, double negation, condition and concessive endings, topic/focus particles, zero arguments, honorification, numeral/counter variants, named entities, quoted speech, antonyms, and cross-sentence attribution. Annotate source–candidate pairs with `meaning_preserved`, `register_preserved`, `naturalness`, `uncertain`, and an error span/rationale. Include intentionally ambiguous cases and expert adjudication rather than forcing binary labels. A pre-registered pilot could start with at least several hundred paired passages across strata and three independent readers per pair, followed by sample-size calculation from the pilot variance; those numbers are **design targets, not completed enrollment**.

Compare `no edit`, current service, style diagnostics without the semantic gate, semantic gate without diagnostics, optional morphology, and the complete engine. This ablation isolates whether diagnostics actually help and whether conservative verification merely suppresses edits. Randomize blinded presentation and score meaning preservation separately from preference. Publish per-stratum intervals, disagreement, over-blocked examples, worst-case error types, and operational latency. Do not tune thresholds on the same examples used for final claims. A human evaluation is required before saying the engine “sounds more natural.”

## Implementation and remaining limits

The prototype is in [Python semantic checks](../../../engine_runtime/super_sejong.py), [quantity normalization](../../../engine_runtime/quantities.py), and [source-anchored TypeScript style findings](../../../src/engine/super-sejong.ts). The service integrates those with [TXT/MD verification](../../../engine_runtime/workflow.py), [Office edit validation](../../../engine_runtime/documents/model.py), and [prompt generation](../../../src/engine/prompt-builder.ts). No external Korean corpus, morphological analyzer, NLI model, or learned naturalness threshold is shipped. The local morphology probe did not resolve transitive packaging rights for every platform, and the corpus audit did not establish a reusable human-written reference set. The current sentence split and regex role analysis can miss paraphrases, ellipsis, quoted scope, lexical antonyms outside the tiny list, and ambiguous topic focus. Cross-document entity continuity, discourse repair, genre identification, and reader-rated quality remain open experiments.

The production rule for these gaps is conservative: preserve the source when the edit cannot be verified, record the failure category without exposing private text, and keep the user in control of the final prose.
