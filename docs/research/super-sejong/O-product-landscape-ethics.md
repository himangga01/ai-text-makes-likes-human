# Super Sejong Engine — Axis O: Product Landscape and Ethics

Research date: 2026-10-03 (Asia/Seoul). Scope: Korean-language AI-text detection, writing assistance, and the implications for a Korean rewriting service. This is a source audit, not a product performance test. Every linked source below was opened during this review. Vendor numbers are reported as vendor claims; no competitor text was submitted for scanning.

## Executive finding

The service should measure *Korean writing quality and meaning preservation*, not whether a detector labels a passage “human.” Detection and revision solve different problems. A detector estimates provenance under a particular training distribution and threshold; a rewriting assistant should help an author express the intended meaning naturally, transparently, and safely. [KatFishNet](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1030/) offers useful Korean linguistic measurements, but its discriminative features are not normative writing rules. [Turnitin's own guide](https://guides.turnitin.com/hc/en-us/articles/22774058814093-Using-the-AI-Writing-Report) says an AI result may misidentify text and must not alone justify adverse action.

The primary [Turnitin AI Writing Report guide](https://guides.turnitin.com/hc/en-us/articles/22774058814093-Using-the-AI-Writing-Report) limits reports to 300–30,000 words of qualifying long-form prose, supported file types (.docx, .pdf, .txt, .rtf), and English, Spanish, Japanese, or Arabic; it cautions that poetry, scripts, code, bullets, and tables are not reliably processed. A Korean passage is outside that detector's stated language scope regardless of length. An external report can therefore be *inapplicable*, not simply high or low scoring.

## Privacy, rights, and academic integrity

Third-party benchmarking requires an explicit data-handling decision. [Originality.ai's security article](https://help.originality.ai/en/article/data-security-16jegvg/), updated 2026-05-14, says scanner uploads may be retained to refine its model; the stated opt-out path is **Account & Settings → Data Opt-Out → turn “Enable Data Usage” off**. This addresses the stated training-use option, not every retention or access question. [DeepL's infrastructure statement](https://support.deepl.com/hc/en-us/articles/26380849099932-DeepL-infrastructure-and-data-protection) describes data handling for paid subscriptions, while [its free-access article](https://support.deepl.com/hc/en-us/articles/18829544756636-Manage-access-to-the-free-version-of-DeepL-for-logged-in-users) explicitly says the free version lacks the same protection; [the Write help page](https://support.deepl.com/hc/ko/articles/6318834492700-DeepL-Write-%EC%A0%95%EB%B3%B4) also warns that a DeepL Pro *translation* plan does not include Write Pro's higher data-security benefit. These are provider statements, not a security audit. The research protocol should use original synthetic or properly permissioned text, record the exact product tier and opt-out setting, and keep customer drafts, personal data, secrets, and unpublished manuscripts out of comparison tools unless the relevant people authorize that specific transfer and the provider terms permit it. Authorization to revise a draft is not automatically authorization to upload it to a competitor.

For academic use, an assistant should preserve the student's argument, avoid fabricating citations or evidence, retain a reviewable edit history when the user wants one, and let the author accept or reject suggestions. Users should follow their institution's assignment-specific rules on permitted assistance and disclosure; this review does not establish one universal academic policy. [UNESCO's guidance](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/guidance-generative-ai-education-and-research?hub=387) emphasizes a human-centered approach and data privacy. [Turnitin](https://guides.turnitin.com/hc/en-us/articles/22774058814093-Using-the-AI-Writing-Report) itself requires human judgment rather than detector-only sanctions. “Undetectable” and “guaranteed human” are therefore scientifically unsupported product promises and poor optimization objectives; the service should not optimize edits against a detector score or imply that assisted text has unassisted provenance.

The opened [ACL paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1030/) is CC BY 4.0 as a publication. Its [linked public repository](https://github.com/Shinwoo-Park/katfishnet) contains code and data, but the publication license does **not** determine whether the underlying texts, code, or hosted corpus permit commercial redistribution or training; no license grant for those uses was established in this review. Likewise, vendor pages establish feature availability, not permission to scrape, clone, or feed customer text into their systems. Dataset provenance, each upstream corpus license, repository licensing, and vendor terms must be checked at their own sources before integration.

## Evidence-grounded Super Sejong opportunities

1. **Expose intent modes.** Offer `correction` (spelling, spacing, particles, agreement with minimal change) separately from `rewrite` (sentence-level stylistic alternatives). This mirrors the meaningful distinction in [DeepL's documented API](https://developers.deepl.com/docs/translate/write-quickstart), but should be implemented independently and tested for Korean quality. The output should state which mode ran.
2. **Use Korean-specific diagnostics as suggestions, not authenticity labels.** Spacing, morphological variation, punctuation, and genre are documented research variables in [KatFishNet](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1030/). Compare candidate passages against genre-matched human judgments for readability and naturalness. Never instruct an author to change a feature solely to reduce a detector score.
3. **Make feature scope explicit.** Publish a feature-by-language and genre matrix, including unsupported cases and minimum useful input length. [Turnitin's matrix](https://guides.turnitin.com/hc/en-us/articles/36917529868429-AI-Tools-with-Turnitin-Clarity) and detector eligibility rules show why one broad “supports Korean” flag misleads.
4. **Prefer edit provenance over detector verdicts.** Present source-to-suggestion spans, reason categories, retained meaning constraints, and author decisions. This supports an accountable revision process without pretending a classifier can identify a writer. Measure accepted suggestions, correction reversions, factual and polarity errors, and human-rated voice preservation.
5. **Keep external benchmarks opt-in and separated from the engine.** If a comparison is ever run, use a written data-use protocol and fixed, licensed, permissioned Korean samples. Report per-genre and per-length TPR at stated FPR, calibration, confidence intervals, and human false-positive cases; do not optimize the engine against a vendor score. [Tufts et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.271/) motivates threshold-specific and shifted-domain testing.

## Research limits

This was a documentation and paper review on 2026-10-03, not a live trial of paid products. The vendor performance values are unreplicated here; no claim of comparative superiority follows. We did not establish commercial reuse rights for KatFishNet's underlying texts or evaluate a Korean proofreading system hands-on. Feature availability, model versions, usage limits, privacy settings, hosting regions, and provider terms are time-sensitive and should be rechecked before any deployment or procurement decision.
