# Super Sejong Engine — meaning-preserving Korean rewriting (research axis D)

Evidence review: 2026-10-03. **Research conclusion:** natural Korean rewriting is a constrained editing and selective acceptance problem, not unconstrained paraphrase generation. Source-anchored edits can reduce the area in which meaning may drift; Korean semantic-similarity, entailment, and register models can provide complementary evidence. None proves equivalence. The proposed Super Sejong Engine design below is **our hypothesis**, not a result established by the cited papers.

## What the primary literature actually supports

| Evidence | Result relevant to the engine | Transfer limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [KLUE: Korean Language Understanding Evaluation, NeurIPS 2021](https://datasets-benchmarks-proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/98dce83da57b0395e163467c9dae521b-Paper-round2.pdf) | KLUE-STS contains 13,224 human-rated Korean sentence pairs on a 0–5 similarity scale; KLUE-NLI contains 30,998 premise–hypothesis pairs labeled entailment, contradiction, or neutral. Sources mix news, reviews, and smart-home queries, hence formal and colloquial styles. NLI is directional; graded STS similarity is not a factual-equivalence certificate. | Sentence-pair benchmarks do not measure whole-document rewriting or certify individual outputs. In the paper's 100-example re-annotation of each NLI set, all four new annotators agreed with the gold label on 71% of KLUE-NLI examples versus 38% of translated KorNLI examples. This is a dataset comparison, not a reliability estimate for a particular KLUE-trained model. |
| [KorNLI and KorSTS, 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.03289) | Korean NLI/STS resources translated from English datasets: training examples were machine translated, and development/test examples were machine translated then professionally post-edited. | The authors note that independently translating paired sentences can change the relation while retaining the English gold label. These resources broaden training data but cannot replace native-Korean, task-specific challenge cases. |
| [Kosmic: Korean Text Similarity Metric Reflecting Honorific Distinctions, LREC-COLING 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.870.pdf) | The authors' KTSEval1k has 1,000 pairs annotated for joint semantic and tonal similarity and formal/informal labels. Their combined metric reached Pearson correlation 0.755 with its human judgments, versus 0.184 for a KLUE-STS baseline **on this tone-sensitive test set**. | The joint score does not isolate factual fidelity from tone; evaluate these dimensions separately. Correlation is not the false-accept rate for dangerous edits, nor a general accuracy claim. A formal-to-informal change can preserve facts and still violate user intent. |
| [MENLI: Robust Evaluation Metrics from Natural Language Inference, TACL 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.tacl-1.47/) | The authors found embedding-based quality metrics vulnerable to information-correctness adversarial edits. NLI-based metrics were more robust; combining them with existing metrics improved robustness in their machine-translation/summarization experiments. | These are not Korean rewriting experiments. The result motivates an NLI signal plus independent checks, not automatic acceptance based on NLI alone. |
| [Right for the Wrong Reasons / HANS, ACL 2019](https://aclanthology.org/P19-1334.pdf) and [content-preservation metric study, ACL SRW 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-srw.23.pdf) | HANS exposed English NLI models' mistaken entailment predictions based on word overlap, subsequences, and syntactic constituents. The metric study tested mutual implication as a paraphrase signal across annotated datasets. | Mutual implication is a useful *test formulation*, not proof that a classifier has inferred correctly. HANS does not quantify a modern Korean model's error rate, so Korean role, scope, and omission probes are still needed. |
| [LaserTagger, EMNLP 2019](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1510/) and [FELIX, Findings of EMNLP 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.findings-emnlp.111/) | Generation can be represented as retaining/deleting input tokens and inserting short phrases; FELIX also models reordering. This yields source-anchored, inspectable edit sequences. | The experiments are in English. Small or structured edits limit the edit surface, but deletion of **안**, a counter, or a case particle can still invert meaning. |
| [Controllable Paraphrase Generation for Semantic and Lexical Similarities, LREC-COLING 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.348/) and [Syntax-Guided Controlled Generation of Paraphrases, TACL 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.tacl-1.22/) | Paraphrase generation can be conditioned on lexical/semantic targets or syntactic form. The former paper explicitly notes that greater surface diversity can degrade semantic similarity. | The demonstrated controls are not a Korean semantic-preservation guarantee. We should target a concrete local defect, not lexical novelty for its own sake. |
| [On the Evaluation Metrics for Paraphrase Generation / ParaScore, 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.08479) | In the paper's studied paraphrase datasets, common metrics aligned poorly with human annotations; reference-free evaluation often outperformed reference-based evaluation. | Its rankings should not be transplanted to Korean without a Korean human-judged comparison. A single reference may undercount valid paraphrases. |
| [Towards Standardizing Korean Grammatical Error Correction, ACL 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.371.pdf) | KAGAS defines 14 Korean grammatical edit types; its alignment and error categories make local changes auditable. The paper distinguishes morpheme-level phenomena such as particles and endings. | Grammatical error correction and style rewriting are different tasks. Learner errors are not evidence that an edited sentence sounds natural to a native reader. |
| [ReproHum meaning-preservation reproduction, 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.humeval-1.19.pdf) and [GEM paraphrase evaluation reproduction, 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.gem-1.54.pdf) | Human judgments can shift with worker pool and evaluation instructions. The latter reproduction reported Fleiss's κ = 0.539 for its semantic-preservation ratings, showing appreciable disagreement. | Neither is Korean-specific. Their methodological lesson is to document instructions, raters, disagreement, and uncertainty rather than treating one mean score as objective truth. |

## Proposed Super Sejong acceptance algorithm

The following is an engineering proposal inferred from the studies above; it needs Korean human evaluation before a performance claim.

1. **Diagnose locally.** Find an explainable problem: awkward connective, repeated ending, translationese collocation, unclear argument reference, or unsuitable register. Record the span, its surrounding sentence and discourse context, and the intended edit category. A sentence that has no concrete defect should remain unchanged.
2. **Build a protected source signature.** Extract entities, dates, quantities with counters/units, quotation boundaries, negation and negative-polarity expressions, modality (possibility, obligation, intention), temporal order, conditional scope, case-marked arguments, and honorific/register markers. Record a source-clause inventory and its alignment to candidate clauses, including splits and merges, so uncovered content can be inspected; sentence counts alone are insufficient. Korean zero arguments and topic markers make argument interpretation uncertain; mark ambiguity instead of inventing a missing subject.
3. **Generate anchored candidates.** Ask for one or more explicit replacements within bounded spans, with immutable surrounding context and an edit budget measured in Korean eojeol/morphemes. Preserve all protected spans by default. A larger change requires a named diagnostic reason and separate evaluation. Source-anchored tagging is inspired by LaserTagger/FELIX; the particular Korean constraints are our design.
4. **Validate in layers.** First enforce structural and exact-content invariants: no unexplained uncovered source clause, unmatched quote, or change in numbers, units, dates, polarity, named entities, argument roles, or conditional scope. Protect literal spans by default, but review explicitly justified equivalent replacements rather than treating every changed token as an error. Normalization may recognize **세 시간** and **3시간** as equivalent while refusing to equate different counters, scopes, or values. Second use Korean STS and NLI as *fallible* signals. For NLI, put the premise on the arrow's left: **source⇒candidate** probes whether the candidate adds unsupported claims; **candidate⇒source** probes whether it omits source content. Require evidence in both directions for an equivalence claim, inspect contradiction/neutral outcomes separately, and do not turn two classifier scores into a proof. Third check register/tone and discourse continuity. Candidate generation and validation should use independent mechanisms when possible; identical model errors can otherwise correlate.
5. **Select or abstain.** Among candidates that pass safeguards, choose the one whose targeted defect improves under a separately defined naturalness/rubric score with the least semantic change. When a guard is inconclusive, show a suggestion for human review or return the source unchanged. Thresholds should be calibrated by genre and risk category; no universal STS/NLI threshold is justified by the literature.

The direction distinction matters for partial rewrites. From *민수는 사과와 배를 샀다*, *민수는 사과를 샀다* may be entailed, yet it omits the pear; the reverse implication fails. From *민수는 사과를 샀다*, *민수는 사과와 배를 샀다* adds a claim; the forward implication fails. Contradiction is only one kind of unsafe edit: omission and unsupported addition may yield **neutral**, so a no-contradiction rule is too weak.

The guard's **false accept** is a meaning-changing output passed as safe: e.g., *그는 서비스를 이용하지 않는다* → *그는 서비스를 이용한다* (negation), *세 명* → *세 번* (counter), or *A가 B를 승인했다* → *B가 A를 승인했다* (roles). A **false reject** is a legitimate improvement blocked: e.g., *세 시간* → *3시간* or a syntactically different yet equivalent formulation. Exact string matching is therefore too rigid, while a high sentence-embedding score is too permissive. Bidirectional NLI can also falsely accept under model error, truncated context, quotation/conditional scope, or discourse-dependent ellipsis. HANS demonstrates that lexical overlap can mislead English NLI models; it is a reason to probe this risk in Korean, not a measured Korean failure rate. NLI is a warning signal, not a proof system.

## Evaluation program required before rollout

Construct a rights-cleared Korean source–candidate corpus spanning informal messages, essays, work reports, academic prose, and long-form documents. Stratify by register, length, topic, and editing difficulty. Keep all variants of one source document in the same train/development/test partition and reserve a later untouched test set. Include professionally edited human writing, existing-service output, and candidates from each proposed engine stage. Public KLUE-STS/NLI and Kosmic are useful *model-development comparators*; a task-specific, held-out Korean rewrite benchmark is required for product claims.

Annotate **valid paraphrases**, **near-miss semantic mutations**, and **genuinely indeterminate cases** separately. Deliberately vary one phenomenon at a time: sentential versus constituent negation, uncertainty/obligation, numerals and Korean counters, case particles and subject/object roles, aspect/tense, conditions, quotations, honorifics, ellipsis/anaphora, clause or sentence deletion, and cross-sentence inconsistency. For each pair, ask whether source entails candidate, candidate entails source, facts and author intent are preserved, register is preserved, and naturalness improves. Let at least three blinded native-Korean raters judge randomized pairs with enough context to resolve reference and scope; record their genre familiarity and disagreements, adjudicate contentious cases, and retain the pre-adjudication labels and inter-rater agreement. Do not force a single equivalence label where the source itself is ambiguous. A separate copy-editing panel should score *necessity of the edit* so a system cannot earn credit by changing already natural text.

Compare the current service against source-anchored edits, structural guards, bidirectional NLI, tone checking, and the full combination. Report each ablation. Primary safety outcomes are **false-accept rate on semantic mutations**, harmful output rate per document, and omission rate. Utility outcomes are false-reject rate on valid edits, accepted-edit naturalness gain, and abstention rate. Keep an enriched challenge set for failure diagnosis and a representative document sample for estimating field rates: the mutation prevalence in the challenge set cannot yield a production harmful-output rate. Report by phenomenon and genre with uncertainty intervals clustered by source document, not only a pooled score; also show the accepted-edit rate and the safety–utility trade-off at each calibrated risk tier. Review production-like long documents because sentence-pair benchmarks miss discourse failures. Prespecify the rubric and thresholds before the untouched evaluation; a model that generates a candidate should not also be its sole judge.

## Data and reuse notes

- The [KLUE official repository](https://github.com/KLUE-benchmark/KLUE) identifies its work as **CC BY-SA 4.0**. Preserve attribution and ShareAlike obligations for adapted benchmark material. Check each component's model and data card before bundling it in the product.
- The [Korean GEC paper](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.371.pdf) says the NIKL learner corpus is research-only with origin citation; the paper describes its released dataset as non-commercial research material. The [authors' request form](https://forms.gle/kF9pvJbLGvnh8ZnQ6) controls access. Their code being public does not grant commercial corpus rights. Do not train or ship on it without separate permission.
- The [Kosmic paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.870.pdf) describes KTSEval1k; a reusable download and license were **not verified** in this review. Cite the result, but do not import rows into the service until terms are confirmed.

**Implementation priority:** begin with source anchoring, protected quantities/polarity, sentence inventory, and abstention. Add learned NLI/tone signals only after constructing and labeling Korean near-miss cases. This priority is a proposed risk-reduction sequence, not an empirical result from the papers.
