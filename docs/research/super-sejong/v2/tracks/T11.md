# T11 report: Korean NLP tools and models for Super Sejong v2, with runtime feasibility

Track T11, 4 October 2026. Host: Apple M4 (10 cores, 16 GB), macOS, CPython 3.11.15. Windows was not measured.

**Evidence labels**
- **[M]** I measured it on this host.
- **[O]** A source opened by T11. Most were opened by me. Two literature sub-researchers I launched opened the translationese, GEC and formality sources; they recorded each as full text or abstract only, and I note abstract-only below.
- **[R]** My reasoning.

I did not modify the repository. I imported `engine_runtime/super_sejong.py` and `quantities.py` read-only, with bytecode writing disabled; the `__pycache__` timestamps (14:55) are older than my run. I did not run codex or claude, did not call port 7882, and did not build anything.

## Answers in brief

1. **Kiwi at runtime needs a persistent worker; per-gate spawning keeps its cost.**
   - Cost of each spawn [M]: 0.93 s cold start and 582 MB peak RSS.
   - The pipeline spawns Python 5–6 times for a one-chunk job: prepare, then 3 restore scripts, then 1–2 verifies.
   - A worker started once [M]: 0.84 s at startup, then 57 ms to analyse a 2.6k-char source plus a 2.6k-char rewrite, measured from Node over stdio. Steady RSS is 510 MB.
2. **python-mecab-ko is the alternative if the spawn-per-gate design stays** [M]: 0.01–0.02 s cold start, 21 MB RSS, 0.08 ms per sentence. Its disambiguation is clearly worse in Kiwi's (author-run) benchmark.
3. **A local Korean NLI cross-encoder, added as an OR-veto to the shipped guards, cut misses on the untuned set from 11/60 to 2/60 with no extra overblock there** [M]. On the code-reviewer set it added 7 overblocks (8/207 to 15/207). Most of those were number-format paraphrases that the deterministic quantity layer already proves equal.
   - Cost: about 10–30 ms per changed sentence pair on CPU through ONNX Runtime, 0.2–0.25 s cold start, and 270–680 MB.
   - The checkpoint I tested has no declared licence. A licence-clean model must be trained or chosen before shipping (see §3).
4. **No commercially usable Korean GEC model or dataset exists for native awkwardness**, and learner GEC is not an awkwardness detector. Scraping Naver, Daum or the Pusan National University checker is unacceptable.
5. **The translationese literature supports suggest-only, context-conditioned rules aimed at the writer's input.** A 2026 preprint finds LLM polishing lowers these marks (§5). Several of the vendor rules turn translationese repair into tense, modality or role changes, which breaks principle 3.

### 1.3 Recommendation for morphology
- Adopt **Kiwi 0.24.x** (pin wrapper and model together; `model_type='cong'`, `num_workers=1`) inside **one persistent analysis worker**. Do not import it in each gate script.
- Fallback if the worker is not adopted: python-mecab-ko for cheap token and POS cues in per-gate scripts.
- Do not adopt Khaiii, Komoran, Okt, KoNLPy, KSS, PyKoSpacing, or `Kiwi.space` as a gate.

## 3. Meaning and paraphrase checks (NLI and STS)

### 3.1 Published evidence
- **KorNLI/KorSTS**, Ham et al. 2020 [O full] https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03289
  - Machine-translated training data; post-edited dev and test sets.
  - Korean RoBERTa-base: KorNLI 82.75 accuracy, KorSTS 83.00.
  - Data is CC BY-SA 4.0 (https://github.com/kakaobrain/KorNLUDatasets).
- **KLUE**, Park et al. 2021 [O full] https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.09680 , https://github.com/KLUE-benchmark/KLUE
  - KLUE-NLI (30,998 native pairs).
  - Test accuracy: KLUE-RoBERTa-base 84.83, large 89.17.
  - In a 100-pair human check, annotators agreed with gold 91.0% of the time on KLUE-NLI vs 64.5% on KorNLI, so KorNLI labels are noisy.
  - Licence CC BY-SA 4.0.
- **Hossain et al. 2020**, EMNLP [O full] https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.732/
  - On pairs where negation matters, RoBERTa dropped to 51.9 (SNLI-trained) and 66.7 (MNLI-trained).
  - NLI does not learn negation reliably, so the **deterministic negation and modality guards must stay in front**.
- **Babakov et al. 2022**, ACL SRW [O abstract] https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-srw.23/
  - Mutual Implication Score (bidirectional entailment) is best for paraphrase detection among 57 content-preservation measures.
- **SummaC**, Laban et al. 2022, TACL [O abstract] https://aclanthology.org/2022.tacl-1.10/
  - Sentence-level NLI aggregated across a document.
  - This is the template for chunk-level checking.
- **MENLI** [O abstract] https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07316 and **TRUE** [O abstract] https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.04991
  - NLI is more robust than embedding similarity to meaning-changing edits.
  - NLI is complementary to other signals.
- **PAWS-X** [O full] https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.11828
  - mBERT on Korean word-order-adversarial paraphrases: 71.7 (zero-shot) to 83.9 (merged training).
  - Korean is clearly harder than English.
- **Kosmic**, LREC-COLING 2024 [O full] https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.870/
  - A KLUE-STS model correlates only ρ=0.184 with human similarity on tone-sensitive pairs.
  - **Embedding similarity cannot see speech-level drift**, so the voice gate must be separate.
- **No Korean negation or adversarial NLI benchmark was found.**

  - lead 1/20 overblock, 0/20 missed.
  - **res1 7/60, 11/60.**
  - code1 8/207, 0/188.
- Models downloaded under `$SP/r5/T11/hf` (1.4 GB total):

  - `Huffon/klue-roberta-base-nli`: klue/roberta-base fine-tuned on KLUE-NLI. **No licence declared** (HF metadata None).

Threshold transfer check: thresholds were tuned on code1 guard-passed preserve pairs and applied to res1. With a 5% budget for added overblock on code1, KLUE 1−min(P_e) gave res1 8/60 overblock and 2/60 missed.

**Why it works: the two layers fail on different things.**
- KLUE-NLI alone missed:

  | Category | Missed |
  |---|---|
  | c_neg_type (안↔못 and similar) | 8/9 |
  | c_obligation | 7/15 |
  | c_modality | 6/19 |
  | c_role | 6/18 |
  | c_bound | 4/15 |

- The guards already catch every one of those categories.
- The guards' res1 misses are scope (2), time (3), tense (3), sentence type (2) and one out-of-scope pair. NLI flags all but two (one sentence-type pair, one tense pair).
- The 7 overblocks NLI adds on code1:
  - 4 are number-format paraphrases (10명→열 명, 5만 원→50,000원, 1만 원→10,000원, 40명→마흔 명), which the quantity layer already proves equal.
  - 3 are 쓸모없이→쓸데없이, a 태어나/자랐다 reorder, and 다시는→두 번 다시.
  - The NLI veto should therefore run after quantity canonicalisation. Proper masking would remove the four number cases; my crude version removed two.

**False-flag floor on real sentences.** Identity pairs (sentence vs itself):
- Blog: 1/300 flagged (0.3%, CI 0.1–1.9%). News: 5/300 (1.7%, CI 0.7–3.8%).
- Mean lengths were 55 and 66 chars.
- Over 50 sentences, a 0.3% per-sentence rate flags about 14% of documents at least once [R].
- Design consequences:
  - Run NLI **only on sentence pairs that changed**.
  - Skip identical and whitespace-only pairs.
  - Act **per sentence** (revert or retry that sentence), never reject the whole document.

**Cost:**

| Configuration | Per pair (both directions) | Cold start / load | Memory |
|---|---|---|---|
| KLUE-RoBERTa-base, torch, ~16-char pairs | 16.5 ms | — | — |
| KLUE-RoBERTa-base, torch, 55–66-char pairs | 28.5 ms | — | — |

- ONNX fp32 output is identical to torch (1110/1110 argmax).
- int8 disagreed with fp32 on 11/1110 argmaxes and 5/555 mutual flags. The union rule with int8 gave res1 8/60 and 2/60, code1 15/207.
- For a 3k-char chunk with about 25 changed sentences, KLUE-NLI adds about 0.3–0.8 s [R from M].

**Limits.**
- The pairs are short and constructed, not production text.
- The res1 categories happen to suit NLI.
- No native rater judged anything.
- The tested checkpoint has no licence.

**Licence path** [R; not legal advice]. Train our own cross-encoder:
- On klue/roberta-base or a permissively licensed Korean encoder, e.g. `kakaobank/kf-deberta-base` (MIT per HF metadata, reported by the sub-researcher).
- Using KLUE-NLI, which is CC BY-SA 4.0. Whether share-alike reaches trained weights is unresolved and needs legal review.
- mDeBERTa is the MIT-clean fallback, but it is slower and overblocked more (12.6% on code1).

## 4. Speech level, formality and style resources (pointers for T2)

- **StyleKQC** (Cho et al., LREC 2022, arXiv 2103.13439) [O full]: 30,000 questions and commands, formal plus informal. Style classifier at 99.58% on templated data. Dataset licence not stated.

- **No Korean speech-level classifier found on Hugging Face** [O, negative search result].
- **Feasibility [M]:** Kiwi finds a sentence-final EF on 97–100% of sentences, so an ending-class voice lock built from Kiwi plus rules is feasible.

## 5. Translationese in Korean

- **이근희 2008**, 번역학연구 9(4) [O full] https://journal.kci.go.kr/kats/archive/articlePdf?artiId=ART001298565
  - 3,780 flagged items in 100 translated science articles: 것(이다) 25.9%, -고 있다 14.6%, padding 13.6%, -들 5.0%, 되다 passive 4.55%, 가지다 4.3%, 에 의하다 3.6%, inanimate subject 2.7%.
  - There is no control corpus.
- **새국어생활 22(1), 2012** (NIKL) [O full]:
  - 김순영 https://www.korean.go.kr/nkview/nklife/2012_1/22_0105.pdf : 가지다→있다, 에 의하여→에게, 들 not always plural, so deleting it can shift meaning.
  - 김정우 https://www.korean.go.kr/nkview/nklife/2012_1/22_0104.pdf : direction-only frequency shifts in translated text (그/그녀, 것, 때문, 가지다, -어지다, -고 있다, long negation).
  - 강주헌 https://www.korean.go.kr/nkview/nklife/2012_1/22_0108.pdf : these are often "not wrong" under 표준국어대사전; style only.
  - 김한식 https://www.korean.go.kr/nkview/nklife/2012_1/22_0106.pdf : commas per sentence are 0.36 in Korean news vs 1.49 in Japanese news.
- **최유숙 2023** [O abstract] https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART002949941 : judge each 번역 투 form by function and context, not origin.
- **MT and post-editing:**
  - 서보현·김순영 2018 [abstract only] https://www.dbpia.co.kr/journal/articleDetail?nodeId=NODE07408846
  - 안미영 2020 [abstract only] https://www.dbpia.co.kr/journal/articleDetail?nodeId=NODE09312201
  - 이상빈 2017 [abstract only] https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART002290302
  - Together: MT errors are mostly meaning and word-order errors; inanimate subjects and passives are MT-sensitive; trainee post-editors under-fix awkward Korean.
- **Lee 2026**, arXiv 2609.07447 (single-author preprint, not peer-reviewed) [O] https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07447
  - 398,296 KCI abstracts. In 2019 human abstracts: 것이다 28.1%, 에 대한 52.6%, 들 57.8%, 되어지다 0.6% of documents.
  - **Model polishing lowered them** (14.0 / 39.0 / 34.0 / 0.0%). Model *translation* from English raised 에 의해 (11–13% vs 8% for polishing).
  - So these marks are not an LLM-rewrite signature. In a Korean-to-Korean engine they belong in **diagnosis of the writer's input**.
- **KatFishNet**, ACL 2025 [O] https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1030/ : 61.0% of LLM essay sentences contain a comma vs 26.3% of human sentences. This agrees with the in-house comma result.
- **Computational translationese detectors** (Guo 2025, Liu 2025, Valentini 2026): none covers Korean.

**Defensible pattern shortlist** (all suggest-only, speech-level-aware, rewritten in the writer's 말투):
1. 이중피동 (되어지다, 보여지다): lowest false-positive risk; exclude 잊혀지다.
2. Animate agent + 에 의해 + passive: exclude legal or instrumental agents (법률에 의해).
3. Abstract object + 가지고 있다: exclude literal possession.
4. Numeral or quantifier + N + 들.
5. 에 있어서.
6. Stacked particle + 의 (에서의, 으로부터의).
7. Comma after topic 는 or after a sentence-initial connective.
8. Sentence-initial 그는/그녀는 as a pronoun: exclude fiction.
- Do not fire on 것이다, 에 대한, -고 있다 or -를 통해 by occurrence alone. They are common in professional human prose.

**Where the vendor rulebook (`quick-rules.md` §A) conflicts with principle 3** [R from O]:
- **A-9** "~에 의해 피동 → 행위자를 주어로" changes voice and role framing; it should stay suggest-only.
- **A-15** "인지 동사 → ~에 따르면 ~이다" turns hedged *suggest/indicate* into an assertion, which is a **modality change**.
- **A-20** "되고 있다 → 추세 단언(심해졌다)" turns progressive into completed, which is a **tense/aspect change**. The shipped guards miss tense changes (3 res1 misses).
- A-3, A-7, A-8 and A-19 fire unconditionally. The literature supports only A-8 as low-risk.

## 6. What the 윤문 엔진 gets wrong or misses in this area [O from worktree]
- **No morphological analysis anywhere.** `references/metrics.py` and `metrics_v2.py` state "No konlpy/bareun/mecab/spaCy … approximate morphological analysis with regex". `grep` finds no analyser, model or NLI in the vendor scripts.
- **No semantic-equivalence signal.** Meaning protection is modality restoration plus regex gates.
- **The per-step process architecture blocks any model-based check.** Every step is a fresh Python spawn (`python-engine.ts` `run()`), so any model costs ≥0.9 s and 0.4–0.7 GB per step.
- **Translationese rules are partly unconditional, and some prescribe meaning-changing repairs** (§5).

## 7. Recommended v2 architecture

| Tier | Component | Mechanism | Budget per job (single-job Mac/Windows server) | Licence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0, deterministic, in process | Protection, sentence split (keep the regex), quantities, negation/modality/role/lexical guards, josa repair, comma rules, CTA presence, speech-level summary | TypeScript or stdlib Python | <0.1 s per chunk | own |
| 1, persistent local worker (`sejong-nlp`, one process) | Kiwi `cong`, `num_workers=1`. Features: EF endings for the voice lock (T2), morpheme-level translationese matches on **input** (suggest-only), typo hints on **input** (NNP/SL excluded), particle and ending cues for the guards | JSON lines over stdio. Started lazily, or pre-warmed at server boot; idle timeout about 10 minutes; restart on crash; per-request timeout; fall back to Tier 0 if unavailable (fail-open to the deterministic gate, never to "pass unchecked") | Startup 0.84 s once. About 60 ms per 2×2.6k chars. RSS about 0.5–0.75 GB | Apache-2.0. Pin wrapper and model ≥0.24.0 together; ship the NOTICE |
| 2, same worker, shadow first | Bidirectional NLI cross-encoder on **changed, aligned sentence pairs only**, after quantity canonicalisation. Flag a sentence if mutual entailment fails and no deterministic certificate applies. In enforce mode: per-sentence revert or conservative retry | ONNX Runtime (MIT) + `tokenizers` (Apache-2.0); no torch at runtime | 10–30 ms per changed pair. +270 MB (int8) or about 680 MB (fp32). Total worker ≤1.3 GB | **Blocker:** need a licence-clean model, either our own KLUE-NLI fine-tune after legal review of CC BY-SA or the MIT mDeBERTa fallback |
| 3, LLM writer | Rewrites only flagged spans or sentences, with short instructions (KoGEC found long rule prompts add little) | Codex/Claude CLI | Unchanged | — |
| Offline only | LLM judge plus human raters for evaluation (T7). KAGAS-style edit typing of every edit as an evaluation and edit-budget metric | — | — | — |

- I recommend **no LLM judge in the runtime gate**. It adds tens of seconds per call, and the writer model would partly be judging itself [R].
- **Rollout:**
  1. Ship the worker with Kiwi features in shadow.
  2. Add NLI in shadow and log `nliFlags` per category (codes only, no text).
  3. Enforce only after shadow logs show the added overblock on real rewrites is at or below the code1 figure (about +3 points) and after native raters confirm the flagged cases.

## 8. Open questions
1. Licence of NLI weights trained on CC BY-SA data (KLUE/KorNLI). A legal call is needed before Tier 2 can enforce.
2. Windows: cold start, RSS and ONNX/Kiwi wheels are not measured there. The wheels exist (kiwipiepy abi3 win_amd64, onnxruntime and python-mecab-ko cp311 win).
3. NLI behaviour on long, restructured sentences (splits and merges) on real rewrites. That needs the T7 benchmark and a sentence aligner; `document_sentence_drift` is a candidate.
4. The NIKL 맞춤법 교정 말뭉치 약정서 terms; whether the NIKL-trained Kiwi model weights carry upstream constraints (the Kiwi NOTICE defers to the providers' terms).
5. Precision of the Kiwi typo hint was judged by me on n=32 samples only; a labelled check is needed.
