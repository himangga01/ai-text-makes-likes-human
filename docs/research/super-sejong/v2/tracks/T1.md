## 1. Headline findings

1. **[M] The gate warns on "no edit" for most human text.** I ran `workflow.verify(source, source)`, i.e. the candidate identical to the source. It returned GATE_WARN for:
   - 81.2% of human KatFish essays (147/181);
   - 93% of human abstracts (93/100);
   - 99.5% of Naver news articles (398/400);
   - 75% of the owner's blog posts (21/28).

   For windows of about 150 characters:
   - 76.3% of human essays (116/152);
   - 88% of human abstracts (73/83);
   - 97.9% of news (329/336);
   - 38.5% of the blog (10/26).

   LLM text warns at about the same rate (essays 91.7%, abstracts 98.6%), so the warning does not separate human from LLM text. Every one of these warnings is P1_STYLE_TARGET.
   - **Consequence [R]:** almost every chunk pays for a second, "much more conservative" model call. The attempt-1 rewrite is thrown away, because attempt 2 starts again from the source. A null edit can never clear the warning.
2. **[M] Short input reproduced: warn → conservative retry → warn.** On the 147-character smoke text (`$SP/smoke.txt`):
   - The identity candidate gets exit 1 with P1 from two metrics: `ending_comma_rate` z 12.19 → 12.19 (the writer's one comma in "삼았으며,") and `comma_segment_length` z 2.45 → 2.45.
   - Attempt 2 accepts with a warning (CHUNK_WARNING_ACCEPTED, then DOCUMENT_REVIEW_RECOMMENDED). This matches the live event stream in `$SP/live.ndjson`.
   - The two metrics pull against each other. Deleting the writer's comma clears `ending_comma_rate` but raises `comma_segment_length` to z 3.95. Adding commas clears segment length but not the ending comma.
   - The only candidate I found that passes deletes the writer's comma **and** inserts four ungrammatical commas ("목표로, 삼았으며", "사용자, 만족도를"). That gives exit 0.
3. **[M] One of the P1 metrics points the wrong way.** `comma_segment_length` (metrics.py) counts a sentence without commas as one whole segment.
   - Human essays: median 10.90 eojeols, and 73% exceed the z>2 threshold of 8.56.
   - LLM essays: median 7.31, and only 16% exceed it.
   - Abstracts: human 84% vs LLM 69% exceed it. News: 100%.
   - KatFish defines the metric per sentence over comma-split segments, and its "usage rate" is commas per morpheme [C: arXiv 2503.00032, section on comma metrics; local copy `$SP/research/B/katfish.txt`, lines 455–560]. The vendor measures eojeols and commas per sentence, but compares against KatFish's human/LLM means (4.35/8.56, 1.13/2.56). Both the units and the definition differ.
   - The "z-score" is (value − human mean)/(half the human–LLM gap); KatFish published no spread. `baseline_v2.json` is entirely placeholder.
4. **[M] The vendor has no speech-level (말투) guard.** The only register check is `checks.colloquial_erased`: at least 3 "~요" endings in the source, and fewer than half survive. I applied synthetic drift to the 28 blog posts:
   - **해요체 → 합니다체:** caught in 0/17 posts when 1, 25% or 50% of the eligible sentences changed; 3/17 when all changed (each catch was colloquial_erased).
   - **합니다체 → 해라체 on every eligible sentence** (597 sentences): 20/28 posts pass. The 8 that were caught were caught only because my crude conversion also broke a modality marker ("필요합니다"→"필요한다").
   - **Hand-made pairs that pass with exit 0:** "알려 드릴게요. 어렵지 않아요." → "알려 드리겠습니다. 어렵지 않습니다."; "정리했습니다. 어렵지 않습니다." → "정리했다. 어렵지 않다."; and the honorific loss "학부모님께서 직접 작성하셔야 합니다" → "학부모가 직접 작성해야 합니다".
5. **[M] The vendor has no CTA guard.** These pass with exit 0:
   - an invented CTA ("…마쳐야 합니다." → "+ 지금 바로 상담을 신청하세요.");
   - "확인해 주세요" → "확인하라".

   "아래 카카오톡 채널로 상담을 신청하세요" → "편하게 상담을 신청하세요" gets only the P0 change-rate warning, because the text is short. Weakening an imperative to "~하실 수 있습니다" is caught only by Super Sejong v1's `modality_changed`.
6. **[M] The vendor's modality machinery only knows 한다/합니다 forms.** It covers 해야 한다/합니다, 수 있다/있습니다 and 것 같다. Ten probe pairs:

   | Probe | Vendor sees the marker? | `restore_modality` reverts? | Super Sejong v1 |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 해야 해요 / 해야 돼요 / 해야 됩니다 → plain | No (0 hits) | No | modality_changed |
   | 필요해요 → 있어요 | No | No | modality_changed |
   | 수 있어요 → plain | No | No | modality_changed |
   | 것 같아요 / 것 같습니다 → plain | No | No | modality_changed |
   | 듯해요 → plain | No | No | **Missed** |
   | 해야 합니다 → plain | Yes | Yes | modality_changed |
   | 수 있습니다 → plain | Yes | Yes | modality_changed |

   So the vendor sees 2 of 10 and reverts the same 2; v1 catches 9 of 10.
   - On the owner's actual posts (82% 합니다체: 1,184 합니다체 sentence endings vs 118 해요체 and 5 해라체) the gap is small. The vendor regex matched 453 times, and my wider pattern found only 1 modal form outside its coverage.
7. **[M] `strip_injected_commas` deletes the writer's own commas.** It treats any sentence that is not verbatim in the source as "new" and removes every comma after a connective ending in it. After a one-word edit at the start of each sentence that has such a comma, it removed:
   - blog: 124 commas in 122 sentences;
   - human essays: 177 in 154 sentences;
   - human abstracts: 81 in 74 sentences.

   That is 100% of the writer's commas in every touched sentence. Its own docstring says writer commas are "불가침". Commas are the writer's choice: the NIKL online Q&A says no rule forces punctuation, and punctuation exists to convey the writer's intention [C: https://www.korean.go.kr/front/onlineQna/onlineQnaView.do?mn_id=261&qna_seq=308432].
8. **[M] Rules that fire on the owner's natural prose and push the model to change its voice.** Rule triggers on the 28 blog posts:

   | Rule | Blog posts triggered | What it prescribes |
   |---|---|---|

   | A-10 ("수 있다" 4+) | 60.7% | |

   - The footer's self-grade awards "A" only for a change rate of 10–25%, which pressures the model to edit natural text. The owner's principle is that "no edit" is a valid result.
9. **[M] Super Sejong v1 bug found along the way.** `engine_runtime/super_sejong.py:_order_swapped` returns True for an identical sentence that contains two 전에/후에 ordering frames. It compares every ordering against every other.
   - Effect: `lexical_polarity_changed` on candidate == source, which forces exit 2 and rollback.

### `prepare_monolith_input.py`
- Call: `--chunk [--portal-auto-route] --run-dir <abs> --genre essay`.
- Input is `01_input.txt`, which it sanitises in place.
- Outputs:
  - `chunk_manifest.json`, version 1. Fields: source_file, source_chars, source_sha256, chunk_count, body_chunk_count, passthrough_chunk_count, route_hint (light, standard, heavy or null), route_reason, processing_mode (single or chunked), warnings[]. - Routing:
  - 15,000 characters or less with route light or standard → one span.
  - Otherwise target 7,000 characters per chunk, cap 9,000, and cut at every heading line.
  - `HEADING_LINE_RE` also counts lines that start with "1. ", "1) ", "가. " or "(주)" as headings.

### Restore step
- Runs in this order on the candidate in place:
  1. `restore_modality.py --before --after --out --json` → stderr `{restored, skipped, details}`;
  2. `strip_injected_commas.py` (never with `--all`) → `{commas_removed, sentences_touched}`;
  3. `engine_runtime/super_sejong_josa.py` → `{repaired}`.
- Any non-zero exit → `ENGINE_OUTPUT_INVALID`.
- For direct text, restore `skipped > 0` counts as a fidelity failure, which triggers a retry.
- Pinned by `tests/unit/python-engine.test.ts`: the restore order and the stderr keys.

### Gate
- Command: `engine_runtime/workflow.py --vendor-root --attempt 1|2 --before --after [--context 01_input.txt]`.
- It calls `verify_gates.main([... "--genre","essay","--json"])` in-process. The genre is hard-coded to essay.
- Stdout is one JSON line:
  - `gate.exit_code`, 0–3, equal to the process exit code;
  - `hardFailures`;
  - `warningReasons{version:1, codes}`, with codes from P0_CHANGE_RATE, P1_STYLE_TARGET, P2_ANTITHESIS, P3_GOLDEN, P4_NUMBER_DROPPED, P5_MODALITY, P5_UNCERTAIN, or UNKNOWN;
  - `policy`;
  - optional `shadowFailures`.
- Hard failures, any of which forces exit 2:
  - from `checks.py`: quote, heading and footnote failures;
  - wrapper checks: number_injected, quantity_changed, number_dropped, name_changed, sentence_dropped;
  - in enforce mode: polarity_changed, modality_changed, lexical_polarity_changed, participant_role_changed, josa_mismatch.
- Decision table: 0 → accept; 3 → error; attempt 1 with exit 1 or 2 → retry; attempt 2 with exit 2 → rollback, with exit 1 → accept-with-warning.
- Upstream exit codes:
  - 1 = P0 change rate in [0.30, 0.50), P1 missed or over-corrected, P2 every antithesis gone (before ≥ 5, after 0), P3 golden failure, or P5 confident modality loss;
  - 2 = change rate ≥ 0.50;
  - 3 = execution error.
- Pinned by `tests/skills/test_gate_reasons.py`, which covers the 29/30/49/50% boundaries and the recorded P1 fixture `tests/fixtures/direct-live-quality.json` (comma_segment_length z 6.21 → 6.17, "미달").

### Reassembly

- It re-attaches each chunk's original leading and trailing whitespace.
- Exit 1 if the SHA of the input mismatches, a span gap exists, a chunk is empty, or (under `--strict`) a chunk's length ratio is below 0.5 or above 2.0. Any of these fails the whole job, not just the chunk.

## 4. Gates and metrics audit

### P0 change rate
- `1 − SequenceMatcher.ratio` over characters (segmented above 8,000 characters). Warns at 0.30, aborts at 0.50.
- Short texts: a single sentence rewrite can cross 30% [M: the CTA-vague and persona probes, each one sentence, both warned P0].
- [R] It clashes with the "strong" and "very-strong" strengths, which ask for broad rewriting. The P0 warning then sends the chunk to the conservative retry, undoing the user's strength choice.
- Keep the 0.50 abort as a runaway guard. Make the 0.30 warning strength- and length-aware.

### P1 style target
- Uses the five metrics above from `metrics.py` against `baseline.json`.
- Biggest false-positive source [M, findings 1–3]:
  - the "z" uses a made-up spread;
  - units mismatch KatFish;
  - `comma_segment_length` is inverted;
  - the genre is fixed to essay;
  - identity can never pass;
  - the metrics conflict with C-11 and with `strip_injected_commas`.
- Drop it.

### P2 antithesis annihilation
- Only when before ≥ 5 and after = 0. Rare; harmless. Could become an informational code.

### P3 golden (`checks.py`)
- hayeot_injection and cliche_injection are increase-only. That is a good design: the edit must not introduce them.
- colloquial_erased is too coarse for 말투 [M, finding 4].
- Quotes, headings and footnotes are promoted to hard failures by the product wrapper. Keep them.

### P4 sentence touch / numbers dropped
- Report only. The product already hard-fails on number_dropped.

### P5 modality
- Aligns sentence pairs (good design), but the regexes are 한다/합니다-centric [M, finding 6]. Super Sejong v1 already covers more.

### Baselines
- `baseline.json`: KatFish human/LLM means only, no spreads.
- `baseline_v2.json`: every cell is placeholder. Only route_hint uses it, and route_hint only decides single versus chunked.

### `verify_change_rate.py`
- Unused.

### Why short inputs warn on every attempt
- Explained in findings 1–2 and reproduced by `short_repro.py`.
- Mechanism:
  1. A short source with any writer comma after a connective ending, or a few long comma-free sentences, gets z > 2.
  2. The conservative attempt keeps the writer's punctuation, so z_after = z_before.
  3. The verdict is "미달", so P1 → exit 1.
  4. Attempt 2 is accepted with a warning. The delivered text is the conservative attempt-2 output, usually unchanged, because the attempt-1 edits were discarded.

## 5. Pre- and post-processing audit

### `sanitize_text`
- Removes zero-width characters, BOM, bidi controls and tag characters; maps NBSP to a space; recomposes only decomposed Hangul.
- Correct and 말투-neutral. Every gate compares against the cleaned text. Keep it.

### `prepare_monolith_input`
- Chunking is lossless and self-checked, with SHA checks. Keep that.
- Problems:
  - the heading regex treats "(주)…" lines and numbered list lines as headings, which forces cuts;
  - the per-chunk metrics block and the chunk header are written but never delivered (Super Sejong v1 now sends position and register through `chunkContext`);
  - route_hint is computed from placeholder or upstream-derived counts and is used only for single vs chunked.

### `restore_modality`
- Reverts a whole aligned sentence to the source when a deontic or hedge marker disappeared.
- This restores the sentence's 말투 too, because it is the source sentence. Beyond that it does not alter endings, except one step: `_strip_restored_cliche` removes a leading "그러므로/따라서/결론적으로/요약하면/정리하면/이를 통해", "지금이야말로" or "바야흐로" from the restored sentence when the model had dropped it.
- It is blind to 해요체 and "것 같습니다" [M: 2 of 10 pairs reverted].
- HEDGE_RE treats ability "할 수 있다" as hedging, so it protects CTA possibility sentences only by accident.
- Safe-by-default skip logic (low similarity, merge or split suspected, reordering, duplicates). Keep that design. Replace the regexes with Super Sejong v1's classes.

### `strip_injected_commas`
- Correctness bug: writer commas are removed in any edited sentence [M, finding 7].
- The regex also matches a sentence-initial "그리고," or "하지만,". NIKL's rule discourages a comma after common connectives but allows it for emphasis [C, search snippet only; I could not open the primary rule page, and kornorms.korean.go.kr did not resolve]. Either way it is the writer's choice [C: NIKL Q&A 308432].
- Fix: count-based and position-based per aligned sentence (section 6).

### `reassemble_chunks`
- Correct.
- `--strict` turns a length-ratio warning (below 0.5 or above 2.0) into a whole-job failure, not a chunk rollback. Prefer rolling back that chunk.

## 6. Conclusions for v2

### Keep
These behaviours must be preserved (they are pinned by tests or are sound):
1. **Manifest and chunking:**
   - version-1 manifest with lossless code-point spans, SHA checks and footnote passthrough;
   - single route for 15,000 characters or less;
   - whitespace re-attachment on reassembly.
2. **Restore and gate interface:**
   - the in-place restore step and its report keys;
   - the gate JSON shape, exit 0–3 semantics and decision table (retry once, rollback on hard failure);
   - every product hard failure: quotes, headings, footnotes, numbers, signed quantities, names, dropped sentences, and v1 polarity, modality, role and josa checks.
3. **Edit-introduced checks:**
   - increase-only checks (hayeot_injection, cliche_injection, injected connective commas);
   - sentence-level modality restore as a deterministic fallback.
4. **Prompt and processing hygiene:**
   - sanitize;
   - the prompt-injection framing and nonce boundaries;
   - no rulebook hints on the retry. Super Sejong already withholds its own hints; v2 must also stop sending quick-rules.
5. **Version contract:** the ENGINE_VERSION-style check (rename deliberately, updating preflight, `skill_gateway`, packaging and `vendor-engine.test.ts` together).

### Drop
1. **The P1 style-target axis, the two baselines and the z-scores.** Reasons: an inverted metric, unit mismatch, invented spread, essay-only genre, and an identity candidate that can never pass. They warn on 75–99.5% of human text and cost a second model call per chunk.
2. **Runtime vendoring of dead files:** the taxonomy, diagnosis-rules, the playbook, `verify_change_rate.py`, the build scripts, the per-chunk metrics files and the route-hint tell counter. Keep them as research references outside the runtime tree.
3. **Footer self-grading A–D** ("변경률 10~25%" for an A) and "S1 잔존 0". They push edits on natural text.
4. **These rules as instructions:**
   - E-2 and E-7 (말투-changing);
   - E-1, A-18, C-2, I-4 (forbidden split, merge or move);
   - A-1, A-3, A-11, A-20, A-24, H-4, D-13, I-1, I-3 (human ≥ LLM, no separation);
   - A-7 and F-4 as "remove";
   - D-14's "human 0" dictionary (falsified on news).
5. **Sending all 61 rules unconditionally, including on the conservative retry.**

### Improve (concrete designs)
1. **Gate invariant: verify(x, x) is OK.** Make it a property test over KatFish, news and the blog. It currently fails for 75–99.5% of documents through P1, plus 1 of about 2,700 through the `_order_swapped` bug. Fix `_order_swapped` to compare the i-th ordering of the source with the aligned i-th ordering of the candidate, not every pair.
2. **말투 guard (hard):**
   - Classify each aligned sentence's final speech level: 하십시오 (니다/니까/십시오), 해요 (…요), 해라 (다/라/자/냐), 해 (어/아/지/야/네/게), or fragment/heading.
   - Also read the subject-honorific -시- and 께서/님, and first-person markers (저/제가/저희 vs 나/내가/우리).
   - Fail if any non-quoted aligned sentence changes level, drops -시- or 께서, or changes person. Repair deterministically by reverting that sentence.
   - Baseline to beat: vendor catches 0/17 posts at 50% 해요→합니다 drift and 0 of 3 hand pairs.
3. **CTA guard (hard):**
   - Detect CTA sentences in the source: request/imperative endings (-세요, -주세요, -십시오, -바랍니다, -하시기 바랍니다), suggestion (-보세요), contact or channel tokens (전화, 카카오톡, 링크, URL, 문의, 상담, 신청), a URL or a phone number.
   - The candidate must keep an aligned sentence with the same force class (imperative/request ≥ suggestion ≥ possibility "-실 수 있습니다") and the same contact tokens.
   - The CTA count must not increase; a new directive sentence fails as cta_invented.
   - Baseline to beat: vendor 0/6 CTA probes; v1 2/6.
4. **Comma stripper fix:**
   - For each aligned (source, candidate) sentence pair, let k = the number of connective-ending commas in the source sentence.
   - Remove only candidate commas at character positions not matched in a `difflib` alignment to the source, and only while the count exceeds k.
   - Exclude a sentence-initial "그리고/하지만/그러나/그런데" comma unless it was newly inserted.
   - Target: 0 of the 124/177/81 writer commas removed in the unrelated-edit probe.
5. **Modality:** replace `DEONTIC_RE`/`HEDGE_RE` in restore and P5 with Super Sejong v1 classes (9 of 10 probes). Add 듯하다/듯해요 and an ability-vs-hedge distinction for "-(으)실 수 있습니다".
6. **Prompt: diagnosis first.**
   - Run deterministic detectors, then send only the rules that fired, with spans, the way v1 style hints work.
   - Split rules into (a) human-norm errors, which may be fixed when confident, and (b) LLM tells, which are suggest-only and genre-gated, with a per-writer baseline where possible (e.g. the owner's own comma rate).
   - Never send rules on the retry or candidate review.
   - Frame the task as "awkward Korean only; no edit is valid", not "remove AI tells".
7. **Change-rate policy:** abort at ≥ 0.50 stays. The 0.30 warning should apply only to the normal strength, and be measured per edited sentence for short inputs.
8. **Genre:** pass the real genre (blog, report, news, essay, Office) or detect it, instead of the hard-coded "essay".
9. **Reassembly:** on a strict ratio failure, roll back that chunk rather than failing the job.

### Missing: what the 윤문 엔진 does not do at all
- **말투:** no per-sentence speech-level, honorific or persona preservation, and no detection of mixed-level drift introduced by the edit.
- **CTA:** no notion of a CTA. It neither preserves strength or specificity nor prevents invention.
- **Awkward-Korean repair:**
  - no agreement (호응) checks (subject–predicate, adverb–predicate, 왜냐하면 … 때문이다);
  - no redundancy (겹말) list, no wrong-particle correction (v1 josa covers only allomorphs), no word-order or ambiguity checks.
  - Probe [M]: "왜냐하면" sentences that do not close with 때문/까닭/이유/-니까/-거든: human essays 30/52 vs LLM essays 2/27 (abstracts 0, news 0/2). So human-norm errors are common in human text and absent from LLM text; a "remove AI tells" engine will never target them. The check's precision has not been verified; some reasons continue into the next sentence.
- **Clarity and flow:** no measure that ties an edit to improved clarity (referent ambiguity, overlong modifier chains on a sentence basis) or flow (connective appropriateness, given/new order), and no requirement that an edit fix a detected problem.
- **Validation:** no blinded human judgement anywhere. Upstream's own rule 6 asks for at least 3 raters and an ICC, and it was never done.
