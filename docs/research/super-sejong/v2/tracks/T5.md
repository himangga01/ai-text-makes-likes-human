## 0. Summary

1. **Population thresholds for flow features fail on the owner's genre [M].** The owner's blog is not LLM-like on connectives or demonstratives. It is silent on almost every LLM-tell flow feature. The v2 flow checks fire about 3 times in 28 posts (1,399 sentences).
2. **The blog looks "AI-like" on the exact rhythm features the 윤문 엔진 prescribes against [M].** These are sentence-length variance and long-sentence absence. The quick-rule E-1 would force sentence merging "per paragraph" in a blog whose paragraphs are 1–3 sentences. The right default is no edit.
3. **Connective features are genre- and register-bound [M].** The broad connective stack does not separate human from LLM essays (3.79 vs 3.70 per 100 sentences). Connective variety points the opposite way from the "vary your connectives" folk advice. LLM essays have more distinct connectives (5.0 vs 2.4 per essay).
4. **As defect detectors, the flow diagnostics have low precision on a hand-checked sample [M].**
   - Connective stack: 7/35 (20%).
   - Abrupt topic shift: 0/25 on the blog.
   - Repeated subject: 4/20.
   - The labeler was one LLM annotator, not a native speaker.
   - **Their real value is as regression guards against the rewriter.**
5. **The shipped gate misses most flow damage [M].** On 28 posts × 2 random draws per mutation:
   - Added transitions: 0/56 detected for each of three transition kinds.
   - Dropped contrast connective: 0/42.
   - Contrast/cause swap: 0/42 and 0/22.

### 2.4 Demonstrative-subject sentences have two different functions [M, hand-typed]

I typed 35 sampled sentence-initial 이는/이것은 sentences (15 LLM, 20 human, from `$T5/samples2_readable.txt`, labels in `handcheck_labels.json`):
- **LLM (n=15):** 10 are evaluative or consequence comments about the whole previous proposition ("이는 …도움이 될 것입니다", "…를 의미합니다", "…를 보여줍니다"). 4 are reason clauses. 1 refers to a single noun phrase.
- **Human (n=20, news and wikipedia):** 12 refer to a quantity or single noun phrase ("이는 …최고치다", "이는 …증가한 규모다"). 5 are reason clauses. 3 are evaluative.
- **Antecedent for replacement:** in none of the 35 was the antecedent safely replaceable by the previous sentence's noun phrase. The antecedent is almost always a whole proposition. A rule that "restores the noun phrase" would invent wording.
- **The specific tell:** the evaluative-predicate regex (의미·시사·보여줍니다·도움이 됩니다·기여·초래…) gives 0.89 per 100 sentences in LLM essays, against 0.0 in human essays and abstracts, 0.02 in news, 0.14 in wikipedia, and 0.00 in the blog. It has high specificity but about 20% sensitivity.

## 5. What the engine may and must not do for flow

### 5.1 Allowed, each only when the meaning and 말투 gates pass [R]

- Delete a repeated sentence-initial additive connective (또한/그리고/아울러/더불어/게다가/나아가) when the same word or relation class appears twice within 3 sentences and the sentences stay adjacent in the same paragraph. Also delete a doubled connective inside one sentence ("또한 나아가").
- Merge two adjacent sentences with 하며/이며/되며 (or 고) when the first sentence's ending converts at the same speech level, both stay in one paragraph, and the second sentence's initial connective is dropped only if the new ending carries the same relation. The shipped gate accepted all 21 synthetic merges.
- Split a run-on only when:
  - the sentence has ≥4 connective endings and ≥30 eojeol;
  - it is not a quotation, list, legal definition or CTA;
  - each piece receives a complete ending at the same speech level;
  - no modality or negation is dropped.
  Hint level only (precision 63% with guards).
- Replace 이는 with the noun phrase only when exactly one noun phrase in the previous sentence is the referent and it is copied verbatim. In the sample this almost never applies, so default to leave.

### 5.2 Forbidden

- Adding any connective, transition, signpost, summary, intro or outro (the shipped gate misses these, 0/56 each).
- Changing a connective's relation class or deleting a contrast marker (0/42, 0/42, 0/22 shipped).
- Reordering sentences or paragraphs, merging or splitting paragraphs, or moving facts.
- Resolving a zero or demonstrative reference by inserting a name or noun that is not in the immediate source.
- Replacing repeated nouns that appear in the title, headings or hashtags (SEO keyword guard).
- Softening, moving or generalising a CTA, or turning it into a transition.
- Pursuing a sentence-length, CV or "burstiness" target, or varying connectives or endings for variety's sake.

### 5.3 Chunk seams

The shipped chunker (`prepare_monolith_input.py`; chunks only above 15,000 chars; target 7,000, max 9,000) forces a cut before every heading line. Its regex also matches numbered lines like "1. …", then packs paragraphs.

Measured with a verbatim copy of the chunker on 400 synthetic long documents built from 6–9 concatenated posts [M]:
- Median chunk size is 574 chars, because numbered headings force cuts.
- Of 604 non-heading ("packing") seams:
  - 8.8% (53, CI 6.8–11.3%) start with a connective.
  - 1.0% (6) start with a demonstrative.
  - 8.1% (49, CI 6.2–10.6%) leave a short unpunctuated line (an unmarked plain-text heading) at the end of the previous chunk, orphaned from its body.
- The shipped prompt tells the model "서두·결말·전환 문구를 새로 만들지 말고, 경계가 잘린 듯 보여도 그대로 둔다" and passes the dominant register. Nothing prevents dropping a seam connective, or inventing a referent for a first paragraph that begins with 그/이/zero, or a duplicate sentence leaking across the seam.

Seam design [R, with Voita et al. 2019 as analogy]:
1. **Read-only context in the prompt.** Pass the last 1–2 paragraphs of the previous source chunk (≤600 chars) and the first paragraph of the next (≤300 chars), clearly marked "참고 전용, 출력 금지". Use source text, not rewrites, so chunks can run in parallel.
2. **Boundary choice.**
   - Treat unmarked short unpunctuated lines followed by a longer paragraph as headings.
   - Move a cut one paragraph earlier when the next chunk would start with a connective or demonstrative, if the chunk stays ≥50% of target.
   - Consider coalescing adjacent small sections, since `check_headings` already protects headings.
3. **Per-seam invariants, source vs output.**
   - The first sentence keeps its initial-connective class and demonstrative starter.
   - There is no new first sentence and no new last sentence (concluding formula).
   - No output sentence has bigram cover ≥0.85 against the context text and <0.5 against the chunk's own source (`context_leak`).
4. **After reassembly.** Check for near-duplicate adjacent sentences across the seam, and compare connective counts in the seam window (last 2 + first 2 sentences) with the source.
5. **Seam violation policy.** Retry the chunk with the violation named, then fall back to the source chunk. The engine already rolls back chunks.

## 6. Proposed v2 flow checks

| ID | Trigger | Action level | Measured | Genre boundary and counterexamples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FLOW_TRANSITION_ADDED | Net new sentence-initial additive, causal or summary connectives in a chunk (source vs output) | Hard gate | Recall 100% (3×56), benign FP 0/49 on B1/B3/B0 | A legitimate rewrite that adds a causal connective for clarity would also trip. Allow only with an explicit override. |
| FLOW_CONTRAST_CHANGED | Count of contrast markers (하지만·그러나·다만·반면·지만·으나·는데도…) differs from source | Hard gate | 100% on 106 contrast-related mutations | Replacing 하지만 with "단," would trip. Broaden the regex. |
| FLOW_STRUCTURE | Paragraph count or order changed, or sentence-order inversion by aligned bigram cover | Hard gate | 94–100% (F4 94%, F5 100%, F8 100%, F9 100%) | The shipped gate catches reorder but misses merge and split. |

| FLOW_SENTENCE_ADDED | Output sentence with bigram cover <0.5 against the source bag | Hard or warn | 36/56 closing-sentence adds | False-alarm rate on real rewrites unknown. Shadow first. |
| FORMAL_REPEAT (hint) | Two formal connectives of the same relation class (or the same word) within 3 sentences | Hint, keep if relations differ | Precision 20% | Essay-genre. Zero hits in the blog. |
| DEM_EVAL (hint) | Sentence-initial 이는/이것은 + evaluative predicate | Hint, regression gate | 0.0 vs 0.89 per 100 (human vs LLM essays) | Safe auto-edit 0/35. Count must not rise vs source. |
| RUNON (hint) | ≥4 connective endings and ≥30 eojeol, outside quotes, lists and unpunctuated text | Hint only | Precision 63% with guards, 40% without | Fires on 1 blog sentence in 1,399. |

**Dropped as triggers [M]:**
- Connective rate or variety (variety points the wrong way).
- Connective-stack breadth.
- Sentence-length CV or flat-rhythm flag (hits the owner's style).
- Abrupt topic shift (0/25).
- Repeated subject noun (4/20).
- "뿐만 아니라", noun triads, ordinal triads outside essays.

**Principle: gate on change relative to the writer's own text, not on population thresholds.** Population values differ across human genres by up to 3×. The owner's own thresholds are the source chunk.

## 7. Flow rubric (1–5), Korean anchors

Rate flow only, given that meaning and 말투 are preserved, as a reader of the intended genre. The overall score follows the lowest dimension (overall ≤ lowest dimension + 1). Dimensions:
- **F1 연결:** relations between sentences are recoverable and markers fit.
- **F2 지시·참조:** every 이/그/zero reference has a clear target.
- **F3 정보 순서:** given-before-new order, and topic moves have a reason.
- **F4 리듬:** sentence lengths and endings suit the genre and there are no run-ons.
- **F5 문단 전개:** each paragraph has one job.

| Score | Descriptor | Anchor passage (constructed; not the owner's text) | What it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 매끄러움 | Read without noticing links. Relations are recoverable with no connective or one needed one. No reference doubt. | "다음 달부터 정기 모임 장소가 바뀝니다. 기존 장소의 대관 계약이 이달 말에 끝나기 때문입니다. 새 장소는 역에서 걸어서 5분 거리라 오히려 오가기 편해질 것입니다. 첫 모임 전에 약도를 보내 드리겠습니다." | Result → reason → effect → follow-up, no connective. |
| 4 대체로 매끄러움 | One or two small redundancies. The reader never stops. | Same passage with "그리고" added before the third sentence. | One deletable connective. |
| 3 읽히나 매끄럽지 않음 | Connective chain or repeated frame is noticeable, or one unclear reference. Meaning passes. Fixing would clearly help. | "다음 달부터 정기 모임 장소가 바뀝니다. 또한 기존 장소의 대관 계약이 이달 말에 끝납니다. 또한 새 장소는 역에서 걸어서 5분 거리입니다. 따라서 오가기 편해질 것입니다. 또한 첫 모임 전에 약도를 보내 드리겠습니다." | "또한" repeated; the reason relation is blurred into addition; rhythm is monotonous. |
| 2 자주 걸림 | Connective misuse makes the logic wrong, or several reference doubts, or too many clauses in one sentence. The reader re-reads. | "다음 달부터 정기 모임 장소가 바뀝니다. 그러나 기존 장소의 대관 계약이 이달 말에 끝납니다. 이는 오가기 편해질 것입니다. 이에 따라 약도를 보내 드리겠습니다." | "그러나" with no contrast; 이는's target is unclear and it does not agree with its predicate; "이에 따라" has no clear source. |

**Do not penalise (score-neutral):**
- One-sentence-per-paragraph checklist style.
- Repeated keyword nouns (title/heading/hashtag).
- A single "다만" exception clause.
- "이는" that restates a reason.
- A CTA closing paragraph.
- Short declaratives under headings.

**Protocol for paired evaluation:**
- Raters see source and rewrite unlabeled in random order.
- Each is rated 1–5, with a preference or "no meaningful difference".
- A rewrite scoring ≥1 below its source is a flow regression.
- At least 3 native raters, with weighted kappa reported.
- To detect a 0.3-point mean gain (SD 0.9, α 0.05, power 0.8), about 70 passages per genre are needed. [R]
- Include a no-edit arm. "No edit" counts as a valid outcome.

## 8. What the 윤문 엔진 gets wrong or misses [M, with the quoted vendor rule text]

- **E-1** (quick-rules.md line 75): "문장 길이 균일 + 100자+ 장문 부재 → 단문 1~2개 + 인접 문장을 이어 만든 장문 1개를 문단마다". On the owner's blog this triggers almost everywhere: 29 sentences ≥100 chars per 1,000 vs the vendor's quoted human 91.3. The blog's paragraphs are 1–3 sentences, so "per paragraph" cannot be satisfied without cross-paragraph merging. It also prescribes the opposite of NIKL's "one core content per sentence". E-4 prescribes 30%+ compound sentences. In KatFish, human essays are the side with more chained clauses (EC 2.98 vs 1.93 per sentence). That is the human-norm violation, not a goal.
- **E-2:** "종결어미 다양화" mixes sentence-final endings. In a 합쇼체 blog (합니다 418, 습니다 378, 입니다 140) that is a 말투 change.
- **A-16:** its (a) branch says "0개 → 명사구 복원" for a pronoun with no antecedent candidate, and its (c) branch says to repeat the noun phrase when 2+ candidates exist. Roh & Lee 2003 says redundant prominent nouns should become pronouns or zeros. In my sample the antecedent is nearly always a proposition. The "불확실하면 유지" guard is the only protection.
- **H-1/H-3:** thresholds ("한 문단 3회+") fit my finding that counts do not separate and only the formal subset does. H-1 was already removed from verification in v2.5, which agrees.
- **Shipped gate:** misses added transitions, dropped or swapped contrast markers, and paragraph merge/split (section 4). The `P1_STYLE_TARGET` warning (`ending_comma_rate` z +4.16 on one example, from a placeholder baseline) asks for a retry on 21/28 unchanged posts. That contradicts "no edit is valid" and costs a model call.
- **Splitters** require whitespace after the terminator (section 2.1).

## 9. Recommendations for v2

1. Add the five hard flow guards (FLOW_TRANSITION_ADDED, FLOW_CONTRAST_CHANGED, FLOW_STRUCTURE, FLOW_DUPLICATE, FLOW_SENTENCE_ADDED) in shadow mode first. Log codes only, as with `shadowFailureCounts`. Collect real false-alarm rates before enforcing the last one.
2. Keep CONNECTOR_RUN but widen to the formal list, and word its hint "keep if the relations differ". Add DEM_EVAL and RUNON as hint-level rules with the guards above.
3. Remove or neutralise vendor E-1, E-2 and E-4 as prompt rules for blog genre. Never set a CV or long-sentence target.
4. Decide whether `P1_STYLE_TARGET` should still force a retry on unchanged text.
5. Implement the seam design: previous/next read-only context, per-seam invariants, unmarked-heading detection, and a context-leak check.
6. Fix the glued-terminator splitter in all three places.
7. Use the 1–5 rubric above for the first paired evaluation. Start with a no-edit arm on the owner's posts, since the expected edit rate there is about 0.2 per 100 sentences.

## 10. Limitations and open questions

- **Single-annotator labels.** All hand-checks are one LLM annotator, no native adjudication, so precision numbers are indicative.
- **Student essays, not blogs.** KatFish "human" essays are high-school student essays, not blog writers. Features that separate there may not transfer.
- **Author bias.** The flow-guard mutation list was written by me and the guard tuned on it. Recall is an upper bound.
- **No real rewrites.** No real LLM rewrites of the owner's posts were available, so false-alarm rates for the guards on real output are unknown.
- **Seam simulation.** The chunk-seam numbers come from concatenated blog posts (synthetic long documents). I did not measure how an LLM behaves at seams.
- **No paragraph data for LLM text.** KatFish has no paragraph breaks, so LLM paragraph-opening patterns were not measured.
- **Regex proxies are weak for run-ons.** The connective-ending proxy has 0.08 recall against Kiwi. A runtime run-on detector without an analyser is very conservative.
- **Open questions:**
  - Does previous-chunk context actually reduce seam damage? This needs an A/B with real model calls.
  - Should adjacent small sections be coalesced?
  - What is the right cut for FLOW_SENTENCE_ADDED on real rewrites?
