# Super Sejong Engine — Research A: Korean Norms, Translationese, Agreement, and Register

Research date: 2026-10-03. Scope: official National Institute of Korean Language (NIKL) materials opened for this study. This is a source-grounded design memo, not a measured claim that any rule already improves the service. Korean examples below are short illustrative test cases; where an example comes from a source, that source is identified.

## Central distinction: normative validity versus editorial fit

NIKL's orthographic rules govern *how accepted Standard Korean forms are written* [S0]. Its public-language handbook additionally recommends clear expression for public communication. Its argumentative-writing rubric evaluates genre suitability. Those are different authority levels: a spelling rule can identify an error; a public-language preference can identify a candidate improvement for its target genre; a rubric for argumentative prose must not be imposed on dialogue, fiction, personal essays, or quotations. The handbook itself separates accuracy (orthography, syntax, paragraph structure) from communicative quality (audience, intelligibility, tone) on pp. 9–10 [S1]. The 2025 rubric separately scores orthographic norms and genre-appropriate register on pp. 70–72 [S2].

The engine should attach to every finding one of `NORM_ERROR`, `GRAMMAR_RISK`, `GENRE_SUGGESTION`, or `MEANING_RISK`. It should never turn a genre suggestion into an unconditional correction. It should preserve quoted text and proper names unless a user explicitly requests editing them.

## Findings and engineering implications

| Finding | Evidence and status | Conservative engine behavior | Counterexample / abstention |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spacing depends on grammatical function, not the surface string. Particles attach to the preceding word; dependent nouns are separated, with expressly permitted exceptions for some units and sequences. | The official *한글 맞춤법* states Articles 2 and 41–43 [S0]; NIKL explains allowed `10 명 / 10명` in a 2026 answer [S3] and `나만큼` (particle) versus `할 만큼` (dependent noun) [S4]. **Norm.** | Require POS and local construction analysis before recommending spacing. Represent both a prescribed and an allowed form as valid. | Do not change every `만큼` to one spacing. `10명` is permitted; labeling it an error is false. `보다` can be an adverb or particle [S3]. |
| Auxiliary verb spacing can have *two* valid forms. | Article 47 gives a conditional allowance for some auxiliary constructions [S0]; NIKL says `만들어 주다` is the principle and `만들어주다` an allowed form for this construction [S5]. **Norm with allowance.** | Report an error only when neither form is valid in the parsed construction. In generation, choose a document convention and keep it consistent. | A regex enforcing spaces before every `주다` would mark valid `만들어주다` as wrong; a regex always joining it would fail in other constructions. |
| Negation is lexical and grammatical content. | NIKL distinguishes preverbal adverb `안` (`안 오다`) from auxiliary `않-` (`오지 않고`) and their spacing [S6]. **Norm and semantic invariant.** | Parse short/long negation, preserve predicate scope and polarity across rewrites, and only suggest `안/않` spelling when the construction is identified. | `안되다` is lexicalized in some senses; `안 되다` has other senses [S7]. A blanket `안` rule will change meaning or mark a valid word as wrong. |
| Subject–predicate agreement, argument structure, and parallel coordination matter more than surface smoothness. | The NIKL public-language handbook gives a mismatched object/passive example (`...명을 선출되었다`) with two valid repairs, warns against elliptical predicates, and pairs coordinated structures at pp. 11–12 [S1]. An NIKL-supported textbook study treats agreement, coordination, particles/endings, and translationese as distinct error classes on pp. 75–76 and 103 [S8]. **Grammar risk; repair is context-dependent.** | Build an event frame with agent, patient, predicate voice, tense, and modality. A candidate must preserve that frame. If agent is unknown, present alternatives rather than inserting one. | `국회의원 10명을 선출되었다` can become `... 10명을 선출했다` or `... 10명이 선출되었다`; these differ in topic and voice, and the missing agent cannot be fabricated. |
| Some apparently missing subjects are normal Korean ellipsis. | NIKL teacher material describes discourse-supported omission of subject and object, and notes flexible but constrained word order, PDF pp. 105–107 [S9]. **Descriptive linguistic fact.** | Only flag ellipsis when reference resolution is genuinely ambiguous in context; do not insert a subject merely because the clause lacks an overt nominative noun. | `벌써 다 읽었구나.` can be complete in conversation [S9]. Adding `너는` may be awkward or change interpersonal force. |
| A particle switch can change discourse meaning while both forms remain grammatical. | NIKL says `이` and `도` are both possible in its delivery example, but `도` adds an additive meaning; it declines to nominate a context-free “more natural” form [S10]. **Semantic choice, not an orthographic error.** | Include topic/additive/contrastive particles in the meaning signature. Require discourse context to change `은/는`, `이/가`, `도`, `만`. | `친구의 물건이 왔다` versus `친구의 물건도 왔다`: the latter presupposes a prior comparable arrival [S10]. |
| Translationese is a genre-sensitive diagnostic, not a list of forbidden strings. | The public-language handbook recommends avoiding awkward `~에 의해 ~되다`, inanimate active subjects, `~에 있다`, and `~에 있어서` for public prose, pp. 12–13 [S1]. A later NIKL report recommends avoiding *unnecessary* passive, causative, and translationese while maintaining accurate word choice and syntax, PDF pp. 149–150 [S11]. **Editorial guidance.** | Emit a localized suggestion when a construction is both matched and semantically replaceable; require the original agent and affected patient to remain in the candidate. Prefer a suggestion UI over automatic replacement. | A passive is useful when the agent is unknown or the patient is the discourse topic (`문이 열렸다`). `연구실은 3층에 있다` expresses physical location; changing every `~에 있다` to `~이다` would be wrong. Technical, legal, and literary uses may be intentional. |
| Long sentences and vague modifiers can impede comprehension, but shortening is not itself a correctness criterion. | The public-language handbook recommends splitting information-heavy public sentences and disambiguating modifier scope, pp. 11–12 [S1]. **Editorial guidance.** | First mark clause boundaries, modifier attachments, and discourse relations. A split is eligible only if every proposition, number, participant, condition, and causal/temporal relation remains expressed. | `5킬로그램 상당의 금 보관함` has two possible attachment readings in the handbook [S1]. The system must ask for intended reading or offer alternatives; it cannot choose one from length alone. |
| Register is a document-level constraint with separate honorific dimensions. | NIKL's argumentative-writing rubric prefers `해라체`, accepts consistent `하십시오체`, and penalizes mixing these or using casual endings *for that genre*, pp. 71–72 [S2]. NIKL teacher material distinguishes subject, object, and addressee honorification, PDF p. 107 [S9]. **Genre-specific guidance plus semantic/pragmatic invariant.** | Infer or ask for genre/audience; record sentence-final style and `-시-`, `께`, `드리다` honorific features separately. Preserve intentional dialogue and quoted speech. Suggest consistency only within comparable narrative spans. | A customer-service note using `해요체` is not automatically wrong. `어머니께서 ... 드리셨다` and `어머니가 ... 주었다` do not have equivalent honorific relations. |

The handbook's extracted PDF text has at least one apparent source/format defect: on p. 13, the example preceding a suggested alternative for `~에 있어서` appears identical to the alternative. We used the unambiguous `품질에 있어서 세계 최고` example instead; no product rule should be induced from that corrupted pair [S1].

## Proposed conservative rule contract

Each rule should return a span, a type, supporting analysis, a candidate (if safe), and an explicit abstention reason. Suggested rule schema:

```text
RuleResult = {
  kind: NORM_ERROR | GRAMMAR_RISK | GENRE_SUGGESTION | MEANING_RISK,
  source_span, construction, evidence_source, confidence,
  candidate?, preserved_features, abstain_reason?
}
```

For rewrite acceptance, preserve at least: named entities, quantities with units, propositional content, agent/patient roles, negation and its scope, tense/aspect/modality, additivity/contrast from particles, and the three honorification targets. These are engineering hypotheses drawn from the cited grammar and guidance, not validated performance claims. A confidence score should express uncertainty; it cannot substitute for checking a changed meaning.

Implement in increasing risk order:

1. **Norm linter:** identify specific particle/dependent-noun/auxiliary constructions with morphological alternatives, recognize allowed variants, and leave ambiguous cases unresolved. Prefer source-linked explanation over silent edits [S3–S6].
2. **Grammar and meaning gate:** compare candidate/source event frames and Korean-specific functional morphemes. Reject polarity, participant, unit, particle-discourse, or honorific changes. For uncertain attachment and ellipsis, abstain [S1, S9, S10].
3. **Editorial recommender:** detect public-language translationese and clause overload only under a suitable genre profile. Generate multiple candidates when interpretation or agent choice is ambiguous [S1, S11].
4. **Document register pass:** compare comparable spans rather than all sentences indiscriminately, excluding quotation and dialogue. Keep subject/object/addressee honorification distinct [S2, S9].

## Evaluation protocol for this axis

Construct contrast sets with an annotation field for `valid`, `invalid`, `context-dependent`, and `genre-dependent`. Include both accepted spellings for Article 47; `나만큼 / 할 만큼`; `이/도` discourse pairs; short/long negation and lexicalized `안되다`; legitimate versus awkward passives; recoverable versus ambiguous ellipsis; three honorification targets; and modifier-attachment ambiguities. Evaluate false positive rate on **valid** expressions separately from recall on clear mistakes. Evaluate rewrite meaning preservation with human bilingual/Korean expert adjudication on the candidate and the preceding discourse, not sentence-level string similarity alone. Report by genre. No corpus sample or model experiment was run for this A-axis memo; quantitative gains remain unverified.

### Limits

These sources are strongest for orthography and public/argumentative prose. They do not establish a universal definition of “human-sounding Korean,” nor do they show that a model can reliably infer intended discourse topic or honorification from a standalone sentence. The proposed rules require corpus calibration and expert error analysis before an automatic rewrite path can be considered safe.
