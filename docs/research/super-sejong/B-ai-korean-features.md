# Super Sejong Engine — Korean human/LLM writing features (research axis B)

Evidence review: 2026-10-03. This note concerns measurable *distributional* differences, not a reliable way to infer the author of an individual passage. A feature that separates classes in one benchmark is neither a grammatical error nor necessarily a desirable rewrite target. **None of the detection studies below validates changing a feature as a way to make Korean writing sound more natural.** For this service, treat these features as hypotheses to test against independent human judgments while preserving the writer's intent, genre, register, and factual content.

## Operational feature definitions

All features require segmentation rules, a frozen morphological analyzer/version, genre labels, and length reporting. Measure both raw and length-controlled values. The figures below are **KatFish corpus measurements**, not target values for rewritten output. See sections 3–5 and Tables 1–3 of the [paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1030.pdf).

1. **Comma use.** Sentence inclusion rate = sentences with at least one comma / all sentences; usage rate = commas / morphemes, averaged at sentence level; relative position = morphemes before a comma / all sentence morphemes. Also record comma-separated segment length and POS-pair diversity around commas. KatFish reports mean per-text essay comma inclusion of **26.31% human versus 61.03% LLM**, poetry **27.01% versus 42.90%**, and abstracts **47.48% versus 65.21%**. Pooled LLM samples include four generators and unequal counts. In the authors' GPT-4o-trained, unseen-model tests, comma-feature logistic regression had mean AUC-ROC **94.88** for essays, **73.10** for poetry, and **75.62** for abstracts (percent scale in paper Table 3); the genre spread is substantial. A comma is appropriate whenever syntax or meaning calls for it: deleting commas to lower a score would be unsound.
2. **Spacing and orthographic status.** With a Korean POS tagger, calculate the fraction of eligible numeral-determiner + bound-noun (MMN–BN), bound-noun (BN), and auxiliary-predicate (VX) boundaries containing a space. Keep the normative status of each boundary separate: bound nouns generally require a preceding space, whereas some auxiliary-predicate constructions permit both forms; KatFish excludes the mandatory no-space `-아/-어 + 지` VX case. Its human samples omitted spaces more often in essays/poetry, with the smallest difference in formal abstracts. Some omissions may be errors rather than natural stylistic choices. Use the finding to prompt **orthographic review**, never to manufacture misspellings or lower the spacing rate toward a corpus average. [KatFish §3.1](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1030.pdf).
3. **Morphosyntactic variety.** For each POS n-gram order 1–5, calculate distinct n-grams / total n-grams, following KatFish. Compute at fixed-length windows or stratify by length because this ratio changes with sample size; report analyzer failure rate and uncertainty. KatFish human essays show higher overall POS-sequence diversity, but its POS diversity immediately around commas is higher in generated essays and abstracts. These are different denominators and should not be conflated. The 2024 essay study additionally suggests measuring dependency-label mix and sentence-length dispersion, but its student/model comparison cannot set service thresholds.
4. **Lexical and discourse variation.** Record length-controlled lemma TTR (or a robust moving-window alternative), lexical density, repeated content lemmas, ending/register transitions, and sentence-length dispersion. These are *hypotheses for new experiments*, not established AI signatures in the sources above. TTR is especially sensitive to text length and morphology; a style optimizer must not force synonym substitution that alters meaning.
5. **Perturbation/regeneration stability.** The 2026 EACL paper defines a local cohesion signal by deleting tokens and scoring change with Korean KoBARTScore; a second signal compares a text with an LLM rewrite and re-rewrite. Its abstract reports up to **0.963 F1 / 0.985 ROC-AUC** on controlled sentence-level detection; these maxima do not measure edit quality or single-text deployment accuracy. Threshold and ensemble-weight selection use an 800-sample tuning subset, with 200 held out; the pseudo-pairing appendix reports no separate performance table. This is a research comparator, not a rewrite rule: it requires model calls and depends on the generation prompts and KoBART scoring model. [Jeon et al. §§4–5 and Appendix F](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-eacl.77.pdf).

## Confounding and experimental design for our service

The corpus design can confound author and source. KatFish human essays come from elementary/middle/high-school AI-Hub writing, poems from age-stratified National Institute of Korean Language material, and abstracts from 2016–2018 language-engineering papers. Their generated counterparts use genre/age instructions; for poems the generator receives the entire human poem, and for abstracts it receives the paper without its abstract. These choices improve topic comparability while introducing source-specific patterns. A pooled model can learn age, time period, punctuation conventions, prompt template, or text length instead of authorship; even accurate authorship detection would not measure naturalness. KatFish Table 1 also has uneven model counts (e.g., 17 Qwen2 abstracts and 61 Llama 3.1 abstracts versus 100 human abstracts). [Paper sections 2 and Limitations](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1030.pdf).

Our evaluation should therefore use **matched writing tasks**, not just class labels. Stratify by genre (informal message, essay, report, academic abstract, presentation), register, author proficiency, topic, document length, and source model/prompt. Split by original prompt/topic, document, and human source text **before** deriving any generated or edited variants; keep all members of a family in the same fold. Include professionally edited human prose, hybrid human+AI prose, older and current generators, and short samples. Report per-stratum sample counts and confidence intervals rather than one pooled AUC. Keep a truly untouched, chronologically later test set and leave-one-model/leave-one-genre evaluations. A detector trained on GPT-4o, as in KatFish's unseen-model setting, must be evaluated separately on Solar/Qwen2/Llama 3.1 rather than treated as a model-independent Korean rule. [KatFish evaluation design](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1030.pdf); [EACL 2026 dataset and limitations](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-eacl.77.pdf).

For the Super Sejong Engine, compare source → candidate edits using human judgments of **naturalness, fluency, register fit, and meaning/fact preservation**, with blinded randomized presentation and multiple native-Korean raters. Measure inter-rater agreement and retain disagreements. Use feature changes only as explanatory covariates. Run ablations for each feature group, and counterfactual checks where the same text is formatted, punctuated, or copy-edited without changing authorship. Include false-positive rates for highly polished human text. Do not market any feature score as proof that a passage was AI-authored. This last design is our proposal inferred from the cross-genre variation and controlled-study limits above; it is not a published performance claim.

## Evidence gaps before an engine rule

1. **Naturalness link is untested.** KatFish and Jeon et al. label text by origin, while the 2024 study compares linguistic distributions. Shim and Baek collect perceived quality, but only for three essays and without testing whether changing any named feature improves the same passage. A paired source/edit study with native readers is needed before adopting a feature as a rewrite objective. [KatFish](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1030.pdf); [Jeon et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-eacl.77.pdf); [Shim and Baek](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART003187971).
2. **Intervention and robustness are untested.** None of these papers shows that adjusting only commas, POS diversity, spacing, or regeneration stability improves a fixed text without changing meaning or register. Recheck each metric after ordinary proofreading, formatting, and human editing, and across short/informal text, hybrid drafts, author proficiency, and current generators. KatFish explicitly excludes hybrid writing; Jeon et al.'s single-text pseudo-pairing has no separate reported accuracy table. [KatFish limitations](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1030.pdf); [Jeon et al. Appendix F](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-eacl.77.pdf).
3. **Implementation validity remains open.** Morphological analyzer choice and sentence segmentation can change POS/spacing counts. EACL's labeled threshold and ensemble-weight tuning must be repeated for each target setting; its reported F1 cannot be treated as calibration for this service. Its §4.1.1 says a higher TOCSIN perturbation drop characterizes LLM output, while its Figure 3 discussion says human text has higher TOCSIN scores; resolve that sign convention against code before implementing the metric. [KatFish limitations](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1030.pdf); [Jeon et al. §§4–6](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-eacl.77.pdf).

## Recommendation for the engine research queue

Prototype a **genre-conditioned research diagnostic** that records these transparent features alongside human ratings of naturalness and fidelity. Investigate a local edit only when readers can identify a concrete readability or cohesion issue and the existing fidelity checks accept the candidate. Treat comma-use distribution as the best-supported *detection comparator in KatFish's setting*, not a naturalness target. Determine whether any feature predicts human preference after controlling for genre, length, source, and topic before giving it an engine threshold or rewrite instruction.
