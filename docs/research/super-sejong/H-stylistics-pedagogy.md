# Super Sejong Engine — Korean stylistics and writing pedagogy (research axis H)

Evidence review: 2026-10-03. The central design finding is **genre and audience before style correction**. An official public-language guide addresses citizen-facing communication; an academic abstract, argumentative essay, report, and personal blog have different purposes. A rewrite should remedy an identifiable communication problem while preserving the writer's claims, social relationship, register, and individual voice. The scoring scheme below is a **proposed engine design**, not a published Korean writing-quality scale.

It demonstrates that endings can carry author signal in that setting, not that an ending fingerprint identifies writers generally. Homogenizing endings may erase a voice cue. |
| [Jang and Park, *LLM-Assisted Writing and Linguistic Homogenization*, 2025](https://www.koredu.net/uploads/papers/ART003286112.pdf) | In a related-prompt, within-study comparison involving 59 students, LLM-assisted writing showed higher overlap among top n-grams and greater embedding-space concentration than deliberative writing; six participants supplied process interviews. | These are group-level text-similarity proxies, not reader-rated quality or a direct test that an individual's voice was lost. | [Kim, *Exploring Principles and Devices for Korean Writing Pedagogy*, 2007](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART001226462) | The author's pedagogical synthesis combines writer, reader, genre, and drafting process and recommends feedback on macro concerns before micro surface features. | An L2 Korean pedagogy proposal, not a model-validated rewrite rubric. It motivates keeping user intent and the communicative task explicit. |

These sources address different constructs. The [NIKL public-language checklist](https://www.korean.go.kr/front/etcData/etcDataView.do?etc_seq=399&mn_id=208) separates grammatical/orthographic accuracy from reader-oriented communication, while the [public-document index](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART001583894) varies communication criteria by genre. Sentence-ending frequencies describe one aspect of addressee relation; they are neither a correctness score nor a measure of authorial voice by themselves. In particular, [NIKL's explanation of *haeyo* and *hapsyo* styles](https://www.korean.go.kr/front/onlineQna/onlineQnaView.do?mn_id=27&pageIndex=1&qna_seq=332328) distinguishes informal-but-polite *haeyo* from formal-polite *hapsyo*. Automatically converting one to the other changes register and may change the social relationship; it does not automatically improve quality.

## Genre-sensitive editorial policy proposed for Super Sejong

The table translates the evidence into **review prompts**. It does not prescribe the proportion of short sentences, frequency of endings, or amount of lexical variation. A genre label is a user preference or an uncertain inference; uncertain cases should receive only high-confidence local suggestions.

| Genre and audience | First questions to ask | Conservative possible edits | Changes that need author confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Citizen-facing notice / public communication** | Can the intended reader find who acts, what action is needed, when, and on what basis? Are specialist terms explained? Is the request respectful? | Repair a broken subject–predicate relation; split a sentence whose action is obscured; explain an acronym on first use when meaning is known. NIKL's public-language guidance applies most directly here. | Altering a legal condition, deadline, eligibility rule, or legally defined term; converting a direct requirement into a weaker suggestion; assuming a specialist term can be deleted. |
| **Internal report / factual briefing** | Are findings, evidence, uncertainty, time frames, and action items recoverable? Does terminology stay stable? | Remove a genuinely redundant connective or repair ambiguous reference while preserving figures, headings, and qualifications. | Replacing a technical label with a casual synonym, compressing caveats, turning a tentative interpretation into a finding, or adding unsupported recommendations. |
| **Academic abstract** | Can readers locate purpose and results? Are methods, scope, and limitations represented as the study warrants? | Flag a missing or obscure purpose/result move for human review; repair local syntactic ambiguity. Academic move research supports *diagnosis*, not mandatory insertion. | Adding a method/result, strengthening a claim, imposing one discipline's structure on another, stripping technical terminology, or changing epistemic hedges. |
| **Argumentative essay / opinion** | Is the position identifiable and connected to its reasons? Are counterpositions and qualifications represented fairly? | Clarify a reference or transition where the reader could misidentify the writer's claim. The TOPIK move study is a weak structural comparator. | Reordering an argument for a template, replacing the author's stance, flattening rhetorical rhythm, or deleting irony, concession, repetition for emphasis, or deliberate fragments. |
| **Personal blog / narrative** | Does the language fit the relationship with readers and the author's established register? Are shifts in endings deliberate and intelligible? | Correct an unambiguous typo or broken agreement; offer optional alternatives for a confusing sentence. | Converting *haeyo* to *hapsyo* or plain style en masse, removing first-person expressions or colloquialisms, standardizing every ending, or replacing a vivid image with generic prose. |

The report, essay, and blog recommendations are **design inferences** from genre and voice evidence above. They need task-specific Korean human evaluation. They should never be presented as NIKL rules for those genres.

## A conservative, implementable diagnostic score

Use a **vector with abstention**, not one scalar called “naturalness.” For source text \(x\), candidate edit \(y\), genre \(g\), and specified audience \(a\):

1. **Eligibility gate:** detect a concrete local problem and attach evidence (e.g., ambiguous antecedent, an unexplained acronym in citizen-facing text, or a broken predicate). If none is located, return “no edit.”
2. **Preservation gate:** compare protected facts, quotations, quantities, negation, modality, and document coverage. Reject candidate edits that alter them without explicit authorization. The meaning-preservation plan is detailed in [research axis D](D-meaning-preserving-rewrite.md).
3. **Genre fit:** score only signals relevant to \(g,a\): public notices prioritize action clarity and reader comprehension; reports prioritize factual traceability; abstracts prioritize recoverable research moves; blogs prioritize local comprehensibility and register continuity. Record orthographic/grammatical accuracy, reader-oriented clarity, and genre fit separately; none substitutes for meaning or voice assessment.
4. **Voice distance:** compare the candidate with comparable nearby *source* paragraphs for sentence-ending distribution, first-person stance, recurring terminology, and characteristic lexical choices. Exclude quotations, dialogue, and sections addressed to a different audience from the comparison. A large change is a **review flag**, not proof of harm; if too little comparable text exists, abstain from a numerical voice judgment. Use matched original-text baselines, not a population-wide ideal style.
5. **Selection:** accept a candidate only if an eligible defect improves, preservation checks pass, estimated genre fit does not worsen, and the voice shift is proportionate to that local repair. Where the genre or the authorial intention is uncertain, return the original with an optional, localized suggestion.

Example auditable fields: `genre`, `audience`, `genre_confidence`, `diagnostic_span`, `diagnostic_reason`, `preservation_flags`, `action_clarity`, `register_shift`, `ending_profile_distance`, `edit_extent`, `decision`. Character/eojeol counts and ending distributions can be computed; “awkwardness,” irony, and intended audience cannot be reliably read from counts alone. **Do not** treat long sentences, Sino-Korean terms, repeated endings, or first-person pronouns as errors by themselves. A report's essential defined term and a blog author's repeated conversational ending are easy false positives.

## Evaluation and data-use discipline

Build a Korean evaluation set with writer-approved source–edit pairs in each genre, including *good unchanged originals*, malformed passages, and edits that are fluent but violate facts, stance, politeness, or voice. Have multiple native-Korean readers judge blind pairs separately for (1) semantic fidelity, (2) improvement of the named defect, (3) genre/audience fit, and (4) preservation of authorial voice; ask the writer to judge the last item where feasible. Report false-edit rate on already-good text and harmful-change rate as primary safety outcomes, plus useful-suggestion rate, abstention rate, and inter-rater agreement by genre. Predeclare the score weights and thresholds from a development set; the cited studies give **no transferable threshold** for this service.

NIKL guides are authoritative normative sources but have distinct reuse terms on their landing pages. KCI abstracts are suitable for source attribution, not for reproducing full articles or assuming datasets are open. The 2025 student study is openly readable as a paper, but its participant texts and code licensing were not verified. Cite findings; do not ingest raw text into a commercial engine without a specific license audit.
