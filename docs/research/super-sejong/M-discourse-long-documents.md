# Super Sejong Engine: Korean discourse and long-document coherence

Research note, 2026-10-03. Scope: Korean connective endings and discourse relations, omitted arguments and reference, and section-to-section consistency in rewriting. This is a research-to-design analysis, not a claim that the present service has a validated discourse parser.

## Evidence map: what the opened primary sources support

| Topic | Direct evidence | What it does **not** establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Korean relation marking | [Lee and Jun (2008), original paper](https://aclanthology.org/Y08-1024.pdf) distinguish abstract coherence relations from their overt markers and construct a *causal* relation ontology using theoretical dimensions plus a substitutability test over Korean connectives and suffixes. Their pilot extracted candidate suffixes from the Sejong corpus. [NIKL writing guidance](https://www.korean.go.kr/nkview/news/91/news6_4.htm) gives concrete cases in which an ill-chosen connective ending makes the relationship between clauses awkward. [NIKL's contextual answer on **-지만** and **-고**](https://m.korean.go.kr/front/onlineQna/onlineQnaView.do?mn_id=216&pageIndex=1&qna_seq=314094) distinguishes a contrary continuation from a listing of coordinate facts. | A surface marker is not a one-to-one discourse-relation label. The 2008 ontology is an exploratory causal subset, not a complete production lexicon. An NIKL Q&A answer is guidance for its context, not a statistical estimate or a blanket ban on a form. |
| Omitted reference | [Roh and Lee (2003), original paper](https://aclanthology.org/U03-1004.pdf) manually recovered omitted elements in 87 Korean texts and found that even in their favorable “cheap Continue” class, 52 of 203 backward-looking centers remained overt. They warn against zero forms that cause ambiguity and explicitly leave omission of other forward-looking entities for future work. [Kwon and Lee (2026), original paper](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-linguistics/article/from-null-pronouns-to-full-nps-exploring-accessibility-markers-in-korean/D6BC512F2D86548A6C46E0C4D1D663F1) report 70.6% subject-antecedent choices for null pronouns in their globally ambiguous experimental condition, while naturalness ratings were high for all three referring-expression forms. [Lee et al. (2025), paper abstract](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.110/) describes 600 manually constructed Korean-to-English translation instances across six phenomena including zero anaphora. | These are corpus patterns, controlled antecedent preferences, and translation challenges, respectively—not a reliable oracle for the intended actor in an individual long document. A majority subject preference still leaves other readings, and high naturalness does not certify a unique referent. The 2025 translation paper's full PDF was inaccessible in this review; no per-phenomenon performance claim is made here. |
| Coreference resources | [Novák et al. (2025), CRAC shared-task paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.crac-1.9.pdf) adds Korean ECMT to CorefUD 1.3 but explicitly states that the Korean source data do **not** annotate zero mentions. The authors also note apparent erroneous merges of distinct entities in Korean ECMT, left uncorrected in the conversion. | A coreference model trained/evaluated on that conversion alone cannot be assumed to recover omitted Korean arguments or perfectly distinguish entities. Reported multilingual scores are not evidence of our engine's accuracy. |
| Coherence modeling | [Barzilay and Lapata (2008), original entity-grid paper](https://aclanthology.org/J08-1001/) represent the distribution of discourse entities and syntactic roles across sentences for local-coherence modeling. [DISRPT 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.447.pdf) separates segmentation, connective detection, and relation classification and reports the latter two remain challenging across its multilingual data. | Entity grids were developed and tested on non-Korean material; grammatical role is not reliably recoverable from a noun's nearest Korean particle alone. DISRPT 2024 does **not** include Korean, so cross-language transfer is a hypothesis. |
| Long-context evaluation | [Steen and Markert (2022)](https://aclanthology.org/2022.coling-1.527/) find that automatic summary-coherence measures do not reliably rank system summaries across all their evaluation measures. [Mujahid et al. (2026)](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1472/) stress-test factual consistency measures on long summaries and report instability under meaning-preserving changes and dense claims. | These papers study summaries, not Korean style rewriting. They motivate contrastive evaluation and evidence-backed checks; they do not furnish a plug-in Korean quality score. |

The sources jointly motivate a **three-layer distinction**. Referential cohesion asks who or what is being discussed. Relational coherence asks how spans relate (cause, contrast, elaboration, condition, time). Document consistency asks whether facts, terminology, viewpoint, and register persist across sections. A text can succeed at one and fail at another: repeated names do not make a false causal claim coherent; fewer explicit pronouns can be natural yet leave a specific reference ambiguous. These layers are an engineering decomposition, not a validated Korean discourse taxonomy.

## Korean-specific analytical issues

### Connection is distributed across endings, adverbs, and context

English-oriented pipelines often look for a sentence-initial connector. Korean can carry the relationship in a predicate ending such as **-고**, **-지만**, **-어서**, **-면**, or **-는데**, in a following adverb such as **그러나** or **따라서**, or in no explicit marker. The [Korean causal-ontology paper](https://aclanthology.org/Y08-1024.pdf) treats the relation as an interpretation of two spans and connects that interpretation to possible lexical realizations. A system should therefore first identify the two propositions and only then assess whether the overt marker fits. Substituting a “varied” connector before establishing the relation risks changing concession into cause or condition into assertion.

Constructed contrast (our example, not corpus evidence): **비가 왔다. 그래서 길이 젖었다.** expresses a plausible cause/result; **비가 왔다. 그러나 길이 젖었다.** creates a contrast expectation that context may or may not justify. Another constructed pair, **비가 왔지만 행사는 열렸다** versus **비가 와서 행사는 열렸다**, shows why swapping an ending to reduce repetition can invert the relationship. A marker-only counter is a useful *review cue*, not an edit command.

### Omission is an option, and antecedents can be uncertain

An omitted subject or object can be interpreted from active topic and event sequence, but that interpretation may remain uncertain even with surrounding text. [Roh and Lee (2003)](https://aclanthology.org/U03-1004.pdf) model local salience and prohibit an ambiguity-inducing zero form; [Kwon and Lee (2026)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-linguistics/article/from-null-pronouns-to-full-nps-exploring-accessibility-markers-in-korean/D6BC512F2D86548A6C46E0C4D1D663F1) show that a preferred antecedent in a controlled choice task is not the only chosen antecedent. In our constructed **민지가 지영에게 파일을 보냈다. 다음 날 회의에서 열었다**, the opener of the file is not fixed by surface form alone. Automatically inserting **민지가** or **지영이** would add a factual claim. A rewrite should preserve the source's unresolved reading when no identity is justified; if it must name an actor, it needs explicit source evidence or should abstain. More context may narrow the candidates, but cannot be assumed to resolve them.

The [CorefUD Korean ECMT caveat](https://aclanthology.org/2025.crac-1.9.pdf) matters operationally: zero-argument evaluation cannot be borrowed from a dataset that omits zero-mention labels. Even overt-mention chains need manual audit for false merges. Entity extraction, zero-reference resolution, and factual relation preservation require separate test sets and error counts.

### Local flow and whole-document consistency are different scales

An [entity grid](https://aclanthology.org/J08-1001/) inspires a conservative document ledger: in which sentence and section do *explicitly named* people, organizations, products, dates, and key terms appear? This can identify abrupt term changes or a value changed in an aligned source/output claim. It cannot establish that the same person still performs the same action: both names may survive a rewrite while the agent changes. It also cannot decide that a section is incoherent merely because a named entity is absent. A value can legitimately differ between a forecast and an actual result, or between a first claim and a correction; compare aligned propositions, time, and attribution before calling a cross-section difference a contradiction. The [long-document factuality stress test](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1472/) shows that model-based scores can shift even when meaning is preserved, making evidence spans and human review essential.

## Proposed Super Sejong document-level diagnostics

The following are **design hypotheses**, not measured product capabilities. Each diagnostic produces a location, source/output evidence, confidence or abstention status, and a suggested editor question. None silently rewrites a document.

1. **Section map and intent ledger.** Preserve section boundaries and headings. Extract per-section main claim, purpose, audience/register, and explicit constraints. Compare a rewritten section with its source before evaluating flow with its neighbors. Avoid summarizing away sections merely to make the document appear concise.
2. **Anchored entity/fact ledger.** Capture exact named entities, amounts, percentages, dates, units, quotations, URLs, and explicit negations with offsets. Align each source claim with its rewritten claim and record actor, predicate, object, time, modality, and attribution where recoverable. Normalize only where equivalence is unambiguous (e.g., date format). Identify candidate omissions, swaps, or conflicting values across sections. A missing repeated mention is a review cue; a changed amount in an aligned claim is a high-priority preservation flag. Matching strings alone do not verify matching roles or referents.
3. **Relation compatibility check.** Segment into clauses/spans; use morphology to identify connective endings and discourse adverbs; classify the *intended* relationship from both spans and wider context. Flag low-confidence combinations such as a causal marker on an apparent contrast. The intervention is “inspect this link,” not “replace **-지만** with **-고**.” Track whether a rewrite changes relation type.
4. **Reference-risk map.** Track overt mentions, candidate aliases, and possible omitted arguments, with evidence locations and the predicate role at each occurrence. Search the local passage plus relevant earlier section anchors; a fixed window can miss a distant introduction or topic reset. Compare the *set of plausible referents* before and after a proposed edit rather than silently assigning one ID to every zero argument. Flag new ambiguity, a changed actor, or an unsupported explicit name; abstain when the source itself leaves identity unresolved. Respect quotations, speaker turns, headings, and section boundaries. This is a risk detector until Korean zero-mention and role accuracy are measured, not a proof of safe omission.
5. **Register trajectory.** Measure shifts in honorific/speech level and terminology across paragraphs, conditioned on genre and quoted voices. Surface sudden shifts for review; allow deliberate dialogue, cited prose, and headings. Repetitive ending profiles may suggest an editorial opportunity, but regularity alone is not a defect.
6. **Candidate comparison with abstention.** Score local fluency only among candidates that pass high-confidence source-to-output checks for aligned facts, polarity, overt entities, and explicit relation markers. Passing those checks does not establish referent or implied-relation preservation. When inferred roles conflict, multiple antecedents remain, or context is truncated, retain the original span and expose the uncertainty for review.

The hierarchy is a proposed evaluation priority: meaning preservation should constrain naturalness edits. No cited source supplies a complete automatic meaning-preservation gate for Korean long documents. A cross-section detector can be useful before full Korean coreference is solved if it confines high-confidence flags to aligned explicit claims and treats inferred entities and relations as questions for review.

## Evaluation design and adversarial contrasts

Construct a rights-cleared, manually annotated Korean test set spanning essays, service messages, reports, fiction, and dialogue. For each item, retain the source, adjacent sections, intended audience, and a minimal corruption. Native Korean annotators independently label (a) whether the document remains coherent, (b) evidence spans, (c) each overt or omitted argument's predicate role and plausible antecedent set (including **underdetermined**), (d) whether the proposed edit preserves that reading, and (e) whether the flag is actionable. Resolve disagreements without erasing them from the research record. Evaluate by genre, length, antecedent distance, number of competing entities, topic shift, and quotation boundary; hold out topics and authors from calibration.

Contrast families should include:

| Positive or licensed variation | Matched failure to catch | Failure mode guarded against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A repeated entity is naturally omitted after introduction. | A rewrite inserts the wrong actor in an omitted-subject clause. | Over-explication / invented antecedent. |
| Two named people remain present with unchanged spelling and one retains the same predicate role. | Both names remain, but the rewrite assigns the action to the other person. | Entity-string agreement masking referent or role drift. |
| **-지만** signals real concession; **-고** lists facts. | An edit swaps these while leaving clauses otherwise fixed. | Changed discourse relation. |
| A later section uses a deliberate abbreviation introduced earlier. | A rewrite changes an organization's name or a product version. | Entity drift. |
| A date or amount is restated in a different but equivalent format. | A date, amount, unit, or negation is changed. | Factual mismatch without false alarms for formatting. |
| Quoted speech legitimately changes speech level. | Narrator register changes unintentionally in one section. | Flattening quoted voices or missing a genuine drift. |
| A section elaborates an earlier claim without a connector. | A “therefore” is inserted despite absent causal support. | Treating connective presence as coherence. |
| A long document reprises a term after several sections. | A middle section contradicts a far earlier constraint. | Limited context windows. |
| A later value is explicitly dated as an update or attributed to another source. | A rewrite changes a value within the same dated, attributed claim. | False cross-section contradiction versus a real source/output change. |

Report precision/recall per error type, evidence-span accuracy, and separate antecedent-set and predicate-role accuracy for overt and omitted arguments. For rewrite selection, report blind preference, human-judged meaning-preservation rate, and abstention rate; do not count a correct risk flag as correct antecedent resolution. Also report false-positive examples in legitimate literary, dialectal, institutional, and learner writing. The key comparison is **baseline engine vs. baseline plus a single diagnostic**, on the *same* source passages. A global score or one example cannot establish value.

## Rights and transfer limits

No paper text, dataset, or annotation was copied into this repository. The [ACL Anthology rights notice](https://aclanthology.org/W04-0205/) marks pre-2016 anthology papers with CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 and research/teaching copying permission; its [post-2016 notice](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.110/) states CC BY 4.0 for those anthology materials. **Paper rights do not automatically license their source corpora, challenge sets, or trained models.** [DISRPT 2024's PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.447.pdf) itself displays CC BY-NC 4.0 and, again, contains no Korean corpus. Check each dataset and original-text license before any commercial training or bundled release. NIKL web guidance was paraphrased and linked; its reuse terms have not been evaluated.

The central transfer limitation is empirical: several algorithms cited above were evaluated in English or multilingual settings without Korean discourse annotations. Their architectures are research inspirations, not validated Korean rules. The 2025 Korean ECMT annotations exclude zero mentions and have documented merge concerns. The Korean zero-reference studies cited here concern local text generation or controlled interpretation, not section-spanning rewrite verification. The Super Sejong Engine should treat omitted-reference resolution and implied relation classification as uncertain until separately annotated Korean evaluation supports them.
