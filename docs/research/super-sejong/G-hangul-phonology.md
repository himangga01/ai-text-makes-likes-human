# Super Sejong Engine — G: Hangul Design, Phonology, and Orthography

**Research status:** 2026-10-03. This is a source-grounded design study, not an implemented feature or a claim that phonological modeling alone produces natural prose. All URLs in the evidence table were opened and inspected. The historical manuscript itself was **not** inspected; the evidence about it comes from UNESCO's description and nomination form and a National Institute of Korean Language (NIKL) scholarly article. Examples below distinguish spelling from pronunciation by placing pronunciations in square brackets.

## Research question and main finding

What does Hangul's design tell a Korean rewriting engine about useful computational units? Hangul is phonemic at the letter level but composed visually into syllable blocks. The 1446 *Hunminjeongeum Haerye* described initial, medial, and final positions and a historically particular set of letters and tone marks. Modern Korean spelling, however, preserves meaningful morphological forms across changes in pronunciation. Therefore a text rewriting engine should use **Unicode grapheme structure for safe indexing, morphology and lexical evidence for orthographic checks, and phonology only for a separately requested spoken-language view**. None of those layers is itself a style or naturalness score. A pronunciation simulator must never directly dictate written replacements. These are engineering implications, not findings directly established by the historical sources. [NIKL Hangul overview](https://www.korean.go.kr/hangeul/principle/001.html); [UNESCO nomination, pp. 2–4](https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/mow001/republic_of_korea_hunmin_chongum.pdf); [NIKL orthography answer](https://www.korean.go.kr/front/onlineQna/onlineQnaView.do?mn_id=&pageIndex=1&qna_seq=327194).

## Historical principle versus modern prescription

The *Haerye* account ties the basic consonant shapes to places/manners of articulation: ㄱ, ㄴ, ㅁ, ㅅ, ㅇ are treated as basic forms; additional strokes derive other consonant letters. Its vowel explanation begins with ㆍ, ㅡ, and ㅣ, historically described through heaven, earth, and person. Initial, medial, and final elements combine into one written syllable block. The original repertoire was **17 initial consonant letters and 11 medial vowel letters**, with historically relevant additional sequences and tone dots. These are descriptions of a fifteenth-century design, not instructions to constrain twenty-first-century writing. [NIKL 2006 article, pp. 9–10](https://www.korean.go.kr/nkview/nklife/2006_3/2006_0301.pdf); [UNESCO nomination, pp. 2–4](https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/mow001/republic_of_korea_hunmin_chongum.pdf).

Modern standard Hangul has a different practical inventory: 19 possible initial letters, 21 vowel letters, and 27 possible written finals (16 simple, 11 complex), with no final consonant as a 28th computational state. The initial ㅇ in a syllable such as 우 fills an orthographic position without an onset sound; it must not be confused with final ㅇ or counted as a spoken consonant. The visually written syllable is therefore useful for display and indexing but cannot be equated with one fixed pronunciation or one morpheme. [NIKL Hangul overview](https://www.korean.go.kr/hangeul/principle/001.html); [Unicode Standard 17.0, §3.12](https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode17.0.0/core-spec/chapter-3/).

Historical tone dots and ㆍ must not be treated as required marks in ordinary present-day Korean. Conversely, a modern-only parser must not erase them from quoted historical texts, names, or philological material. Unicode represents the old tone marks as U+302E/U+302F and permits old syllables to use extended conjoining jamo; the 11,172 precomposed modern syllables are not an exhaustive repertoire of historical Hangul. The sources establish the historical and encoding facts; preservation is an editorial safety policy. [UNESCO nomination, p. 3](https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/mow001/republic_of_korea_hunmin_chongum.pdf); [NIKL 2006 article, pp. 9–10](https://www.korean.go.kr/nkview/nklife/2006_3/2006_0301.pdf); [Unicode §18.6](https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode17.0.0/core-spec/chapter-18/).

Modern spelling is not a narrow phonetic transcript. NIKL's explanation of Article 1 gives the particularly useful contrast 닭고기 [닥꼬기] versus unrelated 닥종이: spelling 닭고기 exposes the noun stem; copying surface sounds would lose that identity. Liaison similarly yields 꽃을 [꼬츨] while leaving the orthography 꽃을. Palatalization yields 붙이다 [부치다], but the written form still reveals 붙- plus causative -이-. The written form is supported by *Hangul Orthography* Article 6; the pronunciation is supported separately by *Standard Pronunciation* Article 17. Both rules depend on morphological context. [NIKL orthography answer](https://www.korean.go.kr/front/onlineQna/onlineQnaView.do?mn_id=&pageIndex=1&qna_seq=327194); [NIKL Article 6 explanation](https://m.korean.go.kr/front/onlineQna/onlineQnaView.do?mn_id=216&pageIndex=1&qna_seq=324560); [NIKL Article 13 answer](https://www.korean.go.kr/front/onlineQna/onlineQnaView.do?mn_id=27&pageIndex=1&qna_seq=319101); [NIKL Article 17 answer](https://www.korean.go.kr/front/onlineQna/onlineQnaView.do?mn_id=216&pageIndex=1&qna_seq=313717).

The two reading studies justify treating a syllable as a potentially useful **research feature** in a model: participants responded to syllable units, initial-syllable frequency, and visual formats in controlled lexical-decision tasks. They do not imply that maximizing frequent syllables, preferring horizontal-looking blocks, or minimizing jamo edit distance improves meaning, naturalness, or readability of full sentences. Kwon et al. explicitly contrast facilitation for the complex nouns they tested with inhibitory or absent effects reported for simpler words. No sentence-level intervention or rewriting outcome was tested. [Pae et al. 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41809-025-00168-8); [Kwon et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/language-sciences/articles/10.3389/flang.2024.1477606/full).

## Implementation invariants and boundaries

| Invariant for a proposed rewrite | Why it follows; what it does **not** establish |
| --- | --- |
| Keep the original code-point sequence and source offsets available throughout analysis; a candidate's displayed span must map back to the original. | Unicode permits canonically equivalent precomposed and conjoining-jamo representations. Safe comparison is possible without silently changing the submitted text. Grapheme boundaries are editing units, not linguistic word or spoken-syllable boundaries. [Unicode §3.12](https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode17.0.0/core-spec/chapter-3/); [UAX #29](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr29/tr29-49.html). |
| Keep NFC equality separate from spelling equality and from meaning equality. Do not treat spacing compatibility jamo as conjoining jamo under NFC. | `각` and `각` are canonically equivalent; a sequence of spacing compatibility jamo such as `ㄱㅏㄱ` has different encoding behavior. NFKC can convert compatibility jamo and even change how neighboring characters join, so it is inappropriate as an unreviewed output transform. [Unicode §18.6](https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode17.0.0/core-spec/chapter-18/); [UAX #15](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr15/tr15-57.html). |
| Check lexical identity and morphological context before accepting any spelling change motivated by a pronunciation rule. If analysis is ambiguous, leave the orthography unchanged and surface the uncertainty. | The ㄷ/ㅌ palatalization spelling rule and the liaison pronunciation rule explicitly depend on what follows the stem. An analyzer can supply evidence but cannot guarantee a unique reading for names, dialect, colloquial quotations, or ambiguous forms. [NIKL Article 6 explanation](https://m.korean.go.kr/front/onlineQna/onlineQnaView.do?mn_id=216&pageIndex=1&qna_seq=324560); [NIKL Article 13 answer](https://www.korean.go.kr/front/onlineQna/onlineQnaView.do?mn_id=27&pageIndex=1&qna_seq=319101). |
| Treat spoken sound and visual syllable statistics as optional diagnostics with genre and audience context, never as an automatic prose preference. | Standard pronunciation rules describe speech; the cited visual-recognition experiments measure isolated-word decisions. Neither supplies a causal estimate of rewrite quality in connected prose. [NIKL Article 17 answer](https://www.korean.go.kr/front/onlineQna/onlineQnaView.do?mn_id=216&pageIndex=1&qna_seq=313717); [Pae et al. 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41809-025-00168-8); [Kwon et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/language-sciences/articles/10.3389/flang.2024.1477606/full). |

## Candidate Super Sejong modules

1. **`SyllableView` (deterministic, non-destructive).** Segment input into Unicode extended grapheme clusters, retain the original string and its offsets in the input encoding, and, **only** for modern precomposed Hangul syllables U+AC00–U+D7A3, expose `(initial, medial, final?)`. Use Unicode's arithmetic decomposition: `index = codePoint - 0xAC00`, initial `floor(index / 588)`, medial `floor((index % 588) / 28)`, final `index % 28` (`0` means no final). For decomposed conjoining jamo, keep the sequence as such and derive a modern block view only when the sequence qualifies; route historic and irregular sequences to an unsupported/preserve path. Also preserve spacing compatibility jamo, emoji, and Hanja. Do not equate an extended grapheme cluster, a precomposed code point, an orthographic syllable, and a spoken syllable: they answer different questions. [Unicode Standard 17.0, §3.12](https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode17.0.0/core-spec/chapter-3/); [Unicode §18.6](https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode17.0.0/core-spec/chapter-18/); [UAX #29](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr29/tr29-49.html).

2. **Canonical comparison and offset map.** Make a separate NFC analysis view for canonical equality, candidate alignment, duplicate detection, and structural comparison. Keep a span map to the **retained original**; normalization can collapse several code points, so normalized offsets alone cannot reconstruct the source. Export the original representation for unchanged spans. Never use NFKC as a silent blanket rewrite: compatibility jamo can become conjoining jamo and alter display or character distinctions. A concatenated string may require normalization again across the joining boundary. [Unicode Standard Annex #15](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr15/tr15-57.html); [Unicode §18.6](https://www.unicode.org/versions/Unicode17.0.0/core-spec/chapter-18/).

3. **Morphology-informed spelling gate.** For a proposed change, identify possible stem, particle, ending, and suffix spans with a Korean morphological analyzer, then check spelling against lexical and normative evidence. Retain competing analyses when context is ambiguous. If a candidate changes only the visible coda or onset to mimic a known pronunciation, flag it for review; do not infer that it is a correction. Homographs, dialect, proper nouns, quoted colloquial speech, and intentional phonetic dialogue can invalidate the flag. Compare 닭고기 → 닥고기, 꽃을 → 꼬츨, 붙이다 → 부치다 as *negative* examples when the left-hand expression and meaning are intended; `부치다` is also a legitimate different word. [NIKL orthography answer](https://www.korean.go.kr/front/onlineQna/onlineQnaView.do?mn_id=&pageIndex=1&qna_seq=327194); [NIKL Article 6 explanation](https://m.korean.go.kr/front/onlineQna/onlineQnaView.do?mn_id=216&pageIndex=1&qna_seq=324560); [NIKL Article 13 answer](https://www.korean.go.kr/front/onlineQna/onlineQnaView.do?mn_id=27&pageIndex=1&qna_seq=319101); [NIKL Article 17 answer](https://www.korean.go.kr/front/onlineQna/onlineQnaView.do?mn_id=216&pageIndex=1&qna_seq=313717).

4. **Dual written/spoken projection, only when requested.** A spoken-script mode may compute a tentative pronunciation view conditioned on morphology and standard pronunciation rules. It could highlight repetition or sequences for *human assessment*, but whether this improves read-aloud quality remains untested; it should not automatically replace spellings with the pronunciation view. Scope must include audience, genre, and dialect. Article 13 is conditioned on a following grammatical morpheme, illustrating why a context-free regex is insufficient. [NIKL Article 13 answer](https://www.korean.go.kr/front/onlineQna/onlineQnaView.do?mn_id=27&pageIndex=1&qna_seq=319101).

5. **Candidate edit provenance.** Record whether a candidate modifies a whole word, a morpheme, a syllable block, or one jamo; annotate whether the change can alter lexical identity, grammatical role, or meaning. A one-jamo edit must **not** receive a low semantic-risk score by default. A spelling change can swap a particle, tense, negation, or named entity despite having small graphic distance. This module is an engineering hypothesis to test, not a rule supplied by the script-design literature.

## Counterexamples and falsifiable checks

| Tempting shortcut | Counterexample / failure | Safer check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Rewrite as pronounced.” | 닭고기 [닥꼬기], 꽃을 [꼬츨], 붙이다 [부치다] would yield nonstandard prose spellings. | Require lexical/morphological spelling evidence; store pronunciation in a separate field. |
| “A syllable block equals a code point.” | Decomposed jamo `각` and the precomposed `각` are canonically equivalent; historical blocks may contain more jamo. | Segment grapheme clusters and preserve an offset map. |
| “All jamo-looking sequences are canonically equivalent.” | Spacing compatibility jamo such as `ㄱㅏㄱ` are not the same NFC case as conjoining `각`; compatibility normalization may change joining behavior. | Classify the encoding first; restrict automatic comparison to canonical equivalence and retain source spans. |
| “A small jamo edit is a small semantic edit.” | `안` versus `않` or `은` versus `는` may change syntax or negate an assertion. | Semantic/morphological checks outrank glyph distance. |
| “Historical vowel/tone conventions are modern style rules.” | ㆍ and tone dots describe a historical writing system and appear in historical quotations; their absence is not a present-day style defect. | Detect historical/quoted spans, preserve them, and apply modern norms only to modern prose. |
| “Frequent syllables sound more natural.” | Controlled lexical-decision latency is not a sentence-level naturalness judgment; frequency effects also vary by word structure. | Compare candidates in blinded human ratings on genre-stratified texts before introducing any frequency feature. |

**Research validation proposal (not yet run):** Construct a small, adjudicated benchmark with five strata: (i) standard written spelling versus plausible surface-pronunciation misspelling; (ii) NFC versus canonically equivalent decomposed conjoining jamo; (iii) spacing compatibility jamo, including sequences that change display under NFKC; (iv) historical/quoted Hangul; (v) real one-jamo meaning changes and legitimate homographs such as 붙이다/부치다. Measure source-span preservation, false-positive correction rate, grapheme-offset correctness, and semantic preservation before/after candidate filtering. An additional blinded Korean-reader study is required before claiming that any syllable-level feature improves naturalness. No code was installed and no product behavior was changed in this research axis.

## Open limitations

The *Haerye* was not directly transcribed or collated for this report, so detailed historical interpretation should be reviewed by a historical Korean linguist. The opened experiments study visual word recognition, not automated rewriting; they support representation choices only weakly. Default grapheme segmentation does not resolve Korean lexical/morphological boundaries or pronounce connected speech. A future implementation must verify legal/technical availability of a Korean morphological analyzer and a licensed dictionary before making normative corrections. The present evidence supports conservative preservation and better candidate validation, **not** a proof of a new optimal Korean prose algorithm.
