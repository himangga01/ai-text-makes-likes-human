# Super Sejong Engine: Korean syntax, information structure, and discourse

Research note F · 2026-10-03 · literature and design hypotheses, **not an implementation or measured product result**

## Research question

How can a Korean rewriting service reduce unnatural repetition and improve the flow of a paragraph without changing who did what, what the writer contrasted, or how two claims relate? A surface rule such as “delete a repeated subject” is inadequate. Korean permits argument omission and constituent-order variation, but neither is a context-free license to rewrite; particles likewise have discourse functions that are not interchangeable. The proposed unit of analysis is an **entity in a clause in a discourse context**, rather than a word or sentence in isolation.

## Evidence and interpretation

| Question | Direct evidence | Consequence for a rewriting engine |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is `은/는` simply an alternative nominative marker? | The [National Institute of Korean Language (NIKL)](https://korean.go.kr/front/onlineQna/onlineQnaView.do?mn_id=216&pageIndex=1&qna_seq=310153) describes `이/가` as a nominative case particle and `은/는` as an auxiliary particle marking topic, contrast, or emphasis. NIKL also says its distribution cannot be reduced to a simple ordering rule. | Do not normalize `이/가` to `은/는`, or vice versa, solely because an entity has appeared before. A particle change can change the utterance's implied alternatives. |
| Is old information always omissible? | [Kim (2000)](https://aclanthology.org/Y00-1018.pdf) argues that realization and movement of previously mentioned arguments depend on their **information function**: focus, link/topic, tail, and contrast. Her analysis of question–answer examples treats functional redundancy, not mere earlier mention, as the driver of omission. | Track what is newly asserted and what is presupposed or contrasted. A repeated entity may need overt mention when it becomes a new topic. |
| Can a centering model guide omission? | [Roh and Lee (2003)](https://aclanthology.org/U03-1004.pdf) studied 87 Korean texts (news, stories, descriptions; about 15 sentences each). Of 203 “cheap Continue” cases, 151 omitted the backward-looking center; of 171 “expensive Continue” cases, 24 did. Their model compares **pairs of successive centering transitions** and avoids omission when it would introduce ambiguity. | Topic continuity is a useful **ranking feature**, not permission to delete. The 52 non-omissions among cheap Continues and 24 omissions among expensive Continues show why deterministic deletion is wrong. |
| Does a zero subject automatically recover the preceding topic or nearest noun? | In an antecedent-choice experiment with 133 Korean-speaking adults, [Choe (2021)](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/landing/article.kci?arti_id=ART002687007) manipulated canonical versus scrambled order and null versus overt pronouns. Its published abstract reports a **subject-antecedent preference for both forms**, including scrambled sentences, with only limited support for the proposed null/overt split. This source was accessible here as a journal-hosted abstract, not a full paper. | Treat grammatical role as one antecedent cue, while preserving competing candidate readings. This preference is an aggregate experimental result, not proof of the intended referent in a particular paragraph or of safe deletion. |
| Is case-marker omission equally acceptable in every position? | In a controlled acceptability study with 60 university participants, [Lee and Choi (2010)](https://aclanthology.org/Y10-1025.pdf) found a stronger preference for overt case marking on focused transitive subjects than on focused objects; object judgments were more sensitive to focus type. | Do not apply one “remove unneeded particles” rule to all noun phrases. Clause role and discourse focus matter. The study concerns specific experimental constructions, not a universal editing rule. |
| Is a topic marker always neutral? | [Wee (1996)](https://aclanthology.org/Y96-1047.pdf) analyzes **contrastive** `은/는` as invoking a larger question and alternatives, sometimes accommodated rather than explicitly stated. The paper explicitly sets aside noncontrastive theme/topic use. | A rewrite that adds or removes `은/는` may create or erase contrast. An engine must distinguish known topic from contrastive topic or abstain when the distinction is unclear. |
| Does flexible order mean constituents can be freely rearranged? | [Kim (2000)](https://aclanthology.org/Y00-1018.pdf) proposes an information-packaging account of movement in question–answer examples; this is a theoretical analysis, not a measured rule for all Korean prose. In an annotated-corpus study, [Shin (2007)](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART001229904) reports dominant basic orders in written text and constraints from argument obligatoriness and semantic role, alongside topic/focus effects. Shin's journal-hosted abstract was accessible here, not the full paper. | Keep clause-level role and semantic-role assignments through any reordering. Do not infer “topic” from left position or “focus” from right position alone, and do not use one analysis of movement as a universal word-order rule. |
| Can connectives be freely varied for “style”? | [Lee and Jun (2008)](https://aclanthology.org/Y08-1024.pdf) distinguish clause-level connective suffixes from discourse-level connective expressions and organize Korean causal markers through relation tests. The [NIKL learner dictionary entry for `-ㄴ데`](https://krdict.korean.go.kr/eng/dicSearch/SearchView?ParaWordNo=85132) shows both connective background use and sentence-final uses; its [`-고` entry](https://krdict.korean.go.kr/kor/dicSearch/SearchView?ParaWordNo=78583) lists enumeration, sequence, continuation, and other constructions. | Retain an explicit source relation unless a replacement is known to preserve that reading in context. String matching on `-고` or `-는데` is insufficient to classify the relation. |
| Are model judgments about endings reliable enough to serve as truth? | [Yu et al. (2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-srw.29/) introduced KoSEnd with 3,000 sentences and 15 ending forms and found model performance improved when the task mentioned that an ending could be missing. The authors warn that most labels relied on LLM annotation and did not establish downstream rewriting quality. | Evaluate ending naturalness, but do not equate an LLM score with native-speaker acceptability or semantic preservation. |
| Can a parser be assumed to solve the problem? | [Oh et al. (2020)](https://aclanthology.org/2020.iwpt-1.13/) manually revised Korean dependency annotations to better reflect morphology and flexible word order; a parser trained on the revised corpus improved labeled attachment score by 3.0 percentage points over its original-corpus counterpart. | Dependency labels are useful evidence but remain fallible. A high-impact rewrite must not depend on a single parse of an ambiguous sentence. |

### Competing accounts and scope

The sources do not establish one universal model. Kim's information-packaging account describes how old arguments change informational roles in selected question–answer contexts; its categorical claims about required deletion or movement are **theoretical proposals**, not editing rules validated across genres. Roh and Lee make a centering and inference-cost proposal for overt versus zero reference, but explicitly leave zero references outside the backward-looking center for future work. Choe reports antecedent preferences in a controlled pronoun-interpretation task; that task does not measure whether deleting an overt noun phrase improves a paragraph. Lee and Choi offer a probabilistic account of **case-particle omission**, a different phenomenon from dropping the entire argument. Wee formalizes only **contrastive** topics. These accounts are complementary at some points but not interchangeable: a system must not treat the statistics of omitted case particles as evidence for omitted subjects, equate every `은/는` with contrast, or equate fronting with topicalization in every construction. The centering study's 87 texts and the case-ellipsis study's university sample constrain generalization across genres, ages, and dialects. [Sources: Kim 2000](https://aclanthology.org/Y00-1018.pdf), [Roh and Lee 2003](https://aclanthology.org/U03-1004.pdf), [Choe 2021 abstract](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/landing/article.kci?arti_id=ART002687007), [Lee and Choi 2010](https://aclanthology.org/Y10-1025.pdf), [Wee 1996](https://aclanthology.org/Y96-1047.pdf), [Shin 2007 abstract](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART001229904).

## Proposed Super Sejong discourse component (new design hypothesis)

The following is an engineering proposal inferred from the literature, **not a reported result of those papers**.

1. **Represent each clause** as a predicate with overt and potentially unexpressed argument candidates, case markers, quoted spans, connective and ending morphology, and source offsets. Keep several parses when role assignment is uncertain. A Korean-aware morphological/dependency analysis is useful here, but the parser's confidence must remain visible. A particle-less noun phrase is still overt; it must not be confused with an omitted argument.
2. **Maintain a short discourse state** for each paragraph: canonical entity IDs, mentions, likely grammatical roles, time since mention, current topic candidate, competing antecedents, and explicit contrast sets. A document boundary, heading, quotation, or speaker change resets or weakens state.
3. **Separate two naturalness opportunities**: repeated overt reference (`철수는 ... 철수는 ...`) and repeated *discourse packaging* (the same topic particle or opening formula). Either is only a candidate. No automatic deletion follows from repetition.
4. **Generate minimal alternatives**: preserve the noun phrase, omit a recoverable subject/object, or repeat a shorter noun phrase. Consider constituent reordering or a particle change only as a separately justified, higher-risk proposal with the same argument roles and defensible topic/focus reading. Prefer the original when two people or two plausible antecedents compete.
5. **Validate a discourse signature** before accepting a candidate: entity–predicate–role links, explicit contrast markers, clause relation type, quotation/speaker boundaries, and the sentence's new asserted content. Reject a candidate if a role swaps, an entity becomes ambiguous, a causal/contrastive relation changes, or the model/parser cannot confidently agree. This check supplements existing lexical and factual fidelity checks; it cannot prove full semantic equivalence.
6. **Expose the decision as a local edit with a reason and confidence**, so a reviewer can see why a candidate was suggested or withheld. Do not present the component as a grammar authority.

One possible omission score is a **calibrated ranking model**, with features for previous mention distance, repeated predicate role, overt topic continuity, next-sentence topic shift, number of competing entities, clause boundary, and genre. Grammatical-role salience and order may help rank antecedents, but neither identifies one with certainty. The score should be learned and calibrated on licensed, human-judged paragraph pairs. No fixed weights are justified by the cited studies. The engine should omit only when a high precision threshold and the preservation checks both pass; otherwise it should keep the original and perhaps offer an optional suggestion. [Roh and Lee 2003](https://aclanthology.org/U03-1004.pdf) motivates discourse-transition features, [Choe 2021](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/landing/article.kci?arti_id=ART002687007) motivates testing grammatical-role salience, and [Kim 2000](https://aclanthology.org/Y00-1018.pdf) motivates explicitly modeling information function. None establishes this scoring design or its threshold.

### Failure cases to include in evaluation

These are **constructed test prompts**, not examples copied from the cited works.

| Source and candidate | Failure being tested |
| --- | --- |
| `민수는 보고서를 열었다. 민수는 계약을 검토했다.` → `민수는 보고서를 열었다. 계약을 검토했다.` | Candidate with one overt person; test whether the omitted subject is recoverable in the full paragraph, rather than assuming it from sentence adjacency. |
| `민수는 지영을 만났다. 지영은 계약을 검토했다.` → `민수는 지영을 만났다. 계약을 검토했다.` | Two plausible human antecedents and an overt topic shift in the source; reject unless wider context resolves the actor and the shift remains explicit. |
| `민수는 지영을 만났다. 민수는 계약을 검토했다.` → `민수는 지영을 만났다. 계약을 검토했다.` | A repeated subject is still a poor automatic-deletion trigger when another person is salient. |
| `사과는 먹었지만 배는 먹지 않았다.` → `사과를 먹었지만 배를 먹지 않았다.` | Contrasted alternatives and topic structure change even though the nouns and verbs remain. |
| `비가 왔다. 그래서 경기가 취소됐다.` → `비가 왔다. 그러나 경기가 취소됐다.` | Causal to adversative relation reversal; must reject. |
| `아이가 웃고 손을 흔들었다.` versus `크고 작은 차이가 있다.` | Identical suffix letters do not imply one discourse relation; avoid simplistic `-고` classification. |
| `김 교수는 학생을 만났다. 그분은 ...` / quoted dialogue with two speakers | Honorific reference and speaker boundaries can affect antecedent resolution; abstain when uncertain. |
| Paragraph separated by a heading or list item | Topic continuity should not automatically cross layout boundaries. |

### Evaluation required before any product claim

Build a small, genre-stratified **human-annotated** set of paragraph pairs: formal reports, customer notices, narrative, and casual prose. Independently label source entity–predicate roles, topic/contrast interpretation, connective relation, referent recoverability after omission, and naturalness. Use at least two native Korean annotators and adjudicate disagreements; report agreement separately for each label because focus and contrast are context-sensitive. Create adversarial pairs with competing antecedents and topic shifts, not only easy repetition examples. Split by source document so nearly identical passages cannot leak across train and evaluation sets. Compare (a) current engine, (b) prompt-only baseline, (c) candidate-ranking component, and (d) candidate-ranking plus discourse-signature gate. Primary safety outcomes should be **referent/role preservation** and connective-relation preservation, followed by blinded naturalness preference; report coverage, abstention rate, and confidence intervals. This is an **evaluation plan**, not an executed experiment. The paper findings justify these contrasts but provide no product-specific effect size. [Roh and Lee 2003](https://aclanthology.org/U03-1004.pdf), [Lee and Choi 2010](https://aclanthology.org/Y10-1025.pdf), [Yu et al. 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-srw.29/).

## Limitations of this note

This is a focused literature synthesis, not a systematic review of all Korean syntax scholarship. The older PACLIC/ALTA papers provide valuable hypotheses but small or constrained evidence. The examples above have not been rated by native speakers. No source here demonstrates that the proposed component improves this service, and no live model or product test was run for this research note. The ACL papers and NIKL answer were read directly. The Choe and Shin findings are attributed specifically to their journal-hosted abstracts; their full papers were not available in this review.
